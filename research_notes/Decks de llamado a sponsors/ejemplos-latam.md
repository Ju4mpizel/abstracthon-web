# Sponsor-call documents in LATAM / Bolivia / comparable community tech events

Research window: local PDFs on disk (required) plus public 2023–2026 packets. Local files were extracted with pdfplumber 0.11.10. `_Carta de Sponsor .docx.pdf` and `_Carta de Sponsor.pdf` are byte-identical (12,482,434 bytes, 13 pages, same text); cited once as the WTM/GDG Santa Cruz carta.

## What do local/comparable docs actually contain (carta vs brochure vs deck)?

### Takeaway
Comparable Bolivian community events sell sponsors with a **kit of formats**, not a single file: a short email, a visual brochure without prices, and a formal carta with a benefit matrix. Abstracthon currently has only internal org notes. Public LATAM hackathon decks exist, but they rarely publish cash prices; Bolivian chapters hide tariffs behind a meeting or DM.

### Inferences
- In Santa Cruz community tech, the working commercial kit is **email (door) + brochure (story, no prices) + carta (SKUs + in-kind + photos + named phones)**. LinkedIn “Call for Sponsors” is a lead magnet, not the offer.
- “Carta” in Bolivia is a dated, `usted`, `Presente.-` letter with an Asunto line; “brochure” is a numbered visual presentation; “deck” in LATAM hackathons is often a public web page. Abstracthon has none of these three sponsor-facing artifacts.
- ETH Bolivia’s on-disk PDF should not be treated as a model sponsor packet: it is a company recruiting a community as a channel. The community’s own sponsor motion is a LinkedIn teaser + private packages.
- Events that already have a global brand halo (Google/WTM/GDG, ETH, Platanus) can put prices behind a meeting. A first-edition student hackathon cannot; it needs the packet in the first email.

### Gaps
- No public PDF of Ethereum Bolivia Buildathon 2026 packages was found; LinkedIn says they exist behind DM.

## How many tiers and what benefits appear in WTM / ETH / similar?

### Takeaway
Local WTM/GDG Santa Cruz sells **three cash tiers (Bs 1,500 / 2,000 / 3,000) plus an in-kind track** for a single large event. Hackathon-native value (named challenge, jury seat, workshop, product/API in the build) appears in LATAM hackathon packets at a much higher price band, and is **not** bundled into WTM’s cheapest SKU. ETH Bolivia does not publish tiers; ETH Latam’s “Large Tier” was quoted at USD 10,000.

### Inferences
- Community **conferences** in this scene (WTM, DevFest) sell visibility ladders: logo → social differentiation → tickets → stage minutes → stand. Community **hackathons** (Coding Latam, hack@latam, ETH Latam) sell a different product: challenge/track, jury, workshop, API in the build, talent list, bounty. Abstracthon’s Bs 500 SKU is trying to sell the hackathon product at conference-community prices, without a ladder.
- Separate **Community/University Partner at $0 or in-kind** is standard (DevFest SC, Coding Latam, Campus Party categories, P2P.me letter). Mixing that with cash sponsors in one undifferentiated “sponsor” label weakens both.
- In-kind is not a footnote: WTM carta gives it equal section status (catering, rooms, merch, A/V). Abstracthon’s “o un premio para voluntarios” is a one-line in-kind, not a catalog.
- Jury seat + named challenge is the expensive SKU in hackathon packets (Coding Latam $5,000; ETH Latam $10,000). Putting both in Abstracthon’s only SKU at Bs 500 underprices the distinctive asset of a hackathon.

## What is strong vs weak in the existing Abstracthon sponsor offer (flat Bs 500)?

### Takeaway
The Abstracthon offer is internally coherent for a ~50-person student hackathon, and the **challenge + juror** mechanic is the right product. It is weak as a commercial document: one price, no matrix, no proof, no audience, no use-of-funds, and it gives away hackathon-exclusive benefits that peers meter at the top tier. Bs 500 is not wildly low per attendee versus WTM, but it is too cheap for a named track challenge and a jury seat.

### Inferences

**Strong**
- The distinctive ask is hackathon-native (reto on a track + jurado), which WTM’s conference matrix does not even sell.
- In-kind alternative (“premio para voluntarios”) matches how Bolivian SMEs actually collaborate when cash is tight.
- Multi-city footprint (SCZ / CBB / ORU) and Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors affiliation are proof points that a carta could use and currently does not.
- Bs 500 is a low-friction first yes for a local shop, academy, or community ally; it is in the same *order of magnitude* as WTM’s floor once you scale by audience size.

**Weak**
- It is not a sponsor-facing document. Tone is operational (“La organización debe coordinar…”), placeholders remain (“Agregar mas,” “SPONSOR NOMBRES,” unconfirmed Sunday hour and web date).
- One flat SKU cannot serve a bank that wants a Finanzas challenge **and** a café that can only donate coffee. WTM solved this with 3 cash + in-kind; DevFest SC with Enterprise/Gold vs Community Partner; Coding Latam with $0 / $500 / $2,500 / $5,000.
- Giving a named challenge **and** a jury seat at the floor price collapses the ladder peers use to upsell. Those two benefits should sit at the top, not in the only box.
- No audience description, no past logos, no “why sponsor” (talent / RSE / product-in-the-build), no use-of-funds, no post-event report, no named commercial contact, no factura note, no negotiation sentence — all of which exist in the WTM kit.
- Prize story is internally inconsistent (Bs 200 in the budget table vs USD 200 + internships + academy month on page 3). A sponsor cannot tell what their money buys.
- “Visibilidad en las redes sociales” is unspecified (group vs individual, pre vs post, stories vs feed). WTM meters this: grupal vs individual, plus video type.
- Volunteer-gift in-kind is mixed into the same sentence as cash, so a company donating stickers appears equal to a company funding a track. Peers split “colaborador / community partner” from “sponsor.”
- Three presencial cities with one Bs 500 ticket creates a fulfillment problem the notes never price (does the brand appear in all seats?).

### Gaps
- No evidence in the internal notes of how many sponsors they hope to close, or whether Bs 500 is per seat or national.
- No list of already-committed sponsors; “SPONSOR NOMBRES” is empty.

### Inferences

## What can Abstracthon copy, and what should it not copy?

### Takeaway
Copy WTM Santa Cruz’s **three-artifact kit and matrix mechanics**, DevFest’s **community-partner vs cash split**, and LATAM hackathon decks’ **challenge / jury / bounty as top-tier products**. Do not copy WTM’s gender/Google identity, ETH/P2P.me’s Web3 commercial ask, inflated audience numbers, or Abstracthon’s current habit of putting the best benefits in the cheapest box.

### Cited Findings
- WTM kit composition that closed (or at least was sent): Gmail + 9-page brochure without prices + 13-page carta with matrix, in-kind, named phones, photo annex. ### Inferences

**Copy**
- The three-file kit: 1-page `usted` email with a named human and cell; 6–10 page visual story **without prices**; 1–2 page carta/table **with prices**, in-kind catalog, and photo or prior-edition proof.
- Three cash rungs plus a separate community/university/in-kind track. Suggested mapping from comparables, not a pricing decision: a floor near current Bs 500 (logo + group social + recap); a mid that adds workshop/mentors/tickets; a top that sells **named track + jurado + prize money**. That assignment of benefits is what WTM/Coding Latam/ETH Latam all do, at different currencies.
- “Cómo se usa el dinero” (comida, refrigerios, merch, mentores, premios) — especially because Abstracthon’s food cap is Bs 1,500 and prize table is still moving.
- Audience segmentation (students vs professionals vs startups) and a why-sponsor pentagon (visibilidad, talento, RSE, networking, liderazgo) in Spanish.
- Named local proof: MLSA, university seats, any confirmed prize partners (Bloomtech, Majup, Joddy) **only after they are confirmed**.
- Factura note, negotiation sentence, and “otras formas de colaboración.”
- For community allies: a P2P.me-style reciprocal one-pager (what we ask / what you get / copy-paste assets), kept **off** the cash matrix.

**Do not copy**
- WTM’s gender-inclusion mission, Technovation 9,000-member claim, or Google program halo as if they were Abstracthon’s. Abstracthon is a student hackathon, not a WTM chapter.
- ETH Bolivia’s Web3-only “inclusión financiera / USDC / protocolos” positioning, or P2P.me’s raffle-and-QR commercial letter, as the voice of Abstracthon.
- Putting challenge + juror in the cheapest SKU (Abstracthon today; the thing ETH Latam meters at USD 10k).
- Inflated or contradictory aforo (DevFest 1,200 vs 300+). Use one number and label it (inscritos vs aforo vs meta).
- A LinkedIn-only “Call 4 Sponsors 📢” with a Google Form and no attached packet — that works for WTM because the packet exists behind the form.
- “Sponsor de Impacto Social” as a badge on every tier if the only delivery is a logo on Instagram.
- Unconfirmed prizes, demo URLs (`abstracthon-demoweb.vercel.app`), and internal to-dos in anything a company sees.
- Campus Party / UNAM-scale stand grids and Elite “name the event after the brand” unless Abstracthon actually has venue control and a production budget to match. UNAM’s published grid starts at MXN 16,000 básico — a different institution and a different ask. — [UNAM esquema](https://computacion.ingenieria.unam.mx/pdf/esquema_patrocinadores_computacion_V1.pdf)
- English-only VC decks (Platanus, hack@latam) as the primary Santa Cruz SME artifact. Keep that tone, if at all, for a cloud/devtools addendum.
