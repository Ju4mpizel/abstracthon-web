# Sponsorship tier models for events (3–4 paid plans)

Context for the report-writer: this note is about **event sponsorship packages**, especially tech/community/hackathons. Large-conference practices are flagged as such. Do **not** invent Abstracthon prices. Abstracthon 2026 is a small in-person Bolivia hackathon (MLSA University Community); an internal org draft used a **flat** ask of Bs 500 or a volunteer prize, plus the right to propose a track challenge and designate a juror. That draft is a starting point to test against general models, not a public price list.

## Why 3 or 4 tiers is the default, and when to use 3 vs 4

### Takeaway
Three to four paid levels is the industry default because it gives budget choice without decision paralysis: fewer than three collapses differentiation; five or more is a large-inventory conference pattern that confuses buyers at community scale. Use **3** for a first-time or small hackathon; add a **4th** only when a scarce title/presenting slot (1–2 seats) is real inventory, not a vanity label.

### Cited Findings
- Guidebook’s event-glossary guidance: stick to 3–4 levels; five or more “confuse sponsors and complicate sales conversations. More options don’t mean more revenue.” — [Guidebook: What are Sponsorship Tiers?](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- SponsorCX: “A range of three to four tiers is right for most organizations. Below three, you lose differentiation. Above four, you create confusion about where to start and where the real value is.” Niche items (naming, presenting, category exclusivity) can sit **outside** the standard ladder as add-ons. — [SponsorCX: Gold, Silver, Bronze and Beyond](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)
- i4a (2026 association-event guide): “Most successful programs rely on three to four levels: more than that creates confusion and decision friction, while fewer can cap your revenue potential.” Their classic four-tier table caps Platinum/Title at 1–2, Gold 3–5, Silver 5–10, Bronze unlimited. — [i4a: Event Sponsorship Packages (2026)](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- Instrumentl (nonprofit galas/events): “Three to five tiers works for most nonprofits… more than five makes the choice feel overwhelming.” Four is “a safe place to start.” Common metal ladder: Bronze → Silver → Gold → Presenting. — [Instrumentl: How to Create Nonprofit Sponsorship Levels](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- MLH’s organizer guide tells hackathons to create a short packet with **three** tiers (“reasonable, moderate, and expensive”) and to note that custom packages are available. — [MLH: Sponsorship Prospectus](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- UK student-hackathon organizers: “Most hackathons have at least 3 different tiers.” First-time projection in their sample: up to 2 top-tier, up to 1 mid-tier, more than 2 low-tier (or even no low-tier if needed, but “advised to have a couple”). — [UK Hackathons: Sponsorship](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- Eventbrite (Feb 2024) treats Bronze/Silver/Gold as familiar buyer language and publishes both 3- and 4-level examples (gala with 4 including Partner; B2B conference Platinum–Bronze; community 10k with 4 including a $100 grassroots tier). — [Eventbrite: Why and How to Create Event Sponsorship Levels](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- Cvent (June 2026): Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum “is easy to understand and works well,” but packages should be structured around sponsor **objectives** (visibility, engagement, market leadership), with “an exclusive premium category for the lead sponsor.” — [Cvent: How to Get Sponsors for an Event in 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors)
- PCMA Convene survey of planners (large association/professional events): majority offer **4 to 9** sponsorship levels; 56% offer category exclusivity for top-level sponsors; sponsors contribute on average **28% of event overall revenue**. — [PCMA: What Drives Event Sponsorships?](https://www.pcma.org/convene-survey-event-sponsorships/)
- Published 3-tier examples: COMSOL Conference 2023 Bengaluru (Bronze / Silver / Gold); DevFest Guadalajara 2026 (Oro / Plata / Bronce, Gold limited to 3). — [COMSOL 2023 Bengaluru prospectus](https://cdn.comsol.com/conference/2023/documents/pdf/COMSOL_Conference_SponsorProspectus_A4-Bengaluru.pdf); [DevFest Guadalajara 2026 sponsor deck](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
- Published 4-tier examples: VTHacks 11 (Bronze $3k / Silver $6k / Gold $8k / Diamond $11k); Eventbrite’s contractors-conference template (Platinum $10k / Gold $5k / Silver $1k / Bronze $500). — [VTHacks 11 prospectus](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf); [Eventbrite sponsorship levels](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- Large-conference exception: FIRST Conference 2023 sold **six** paid rungs (Diamond $45k through Exhibitor $6k). That inventory (exhibit hall, private meeting rooms, press conference, opening address) does not exist at a campus hackathon. — [FIRST 2023 sponsorship details](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
- SponsorCX notes the metal names themselves are optional and “generic”; alternatives (Presenting / Official / Supporting, or mission-themed names) still keep 3–4 rungs. — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)

### Inferences
- **3 tiers** is the hackathon/community default (MLH, UK organizers, DevFest GDL, GDG/WTM Santa Cruz’s 2026 cash ladder). It matches a small sales team and a short prospectus (MLH: 2–3 pages).
- **4 tiers** is justified when you can **cap the top at 1–2** and the extra rung is a different *job* (Title/Presenting = ownership/naming; Gold = activation; Silver = presence; Bronze = logo-only), not “slightly bigger logo.”
- **5–9 tiers** is a large-conference / association pattern (PCMA, FIRST). Do not copy it for a first Bolivia hackathon: inventory (stage minutes, booths, data products) cannot support that many distinct products.
- Abstracthon-scale inference: start with 3 paid rungs, or 3 + one separately sold Title/Presenting that is not named Bronze-equivalent. A 4th metal name without scarce inventory will not sell; it will just make Bronze look like the “real” price.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 academic study was found that A/B tests 3 vs 4 tiers on close rate; the 3–4 rule is practitioner consensus, not experimental evidence.
- No public Abstracthon prospectus exists; the Bs 500 flat draft is internal, not a market-tested tier model.
- Campus career-fair rate cards for Bolivia/LATAM with 3–4 priced tiers were not found as public PDFs in this pass (DevFest GDL is the closest public LATAM tech deck).

## How should benefits ladder (each tier includes the one below vs a la carte)?

### Takeaway
Default is a **stacked ladder**: each higher tier includes everything below plus at least one gated, scarce benefit. Pure a la carte is for enterprise buyers and burns sales time; the hybrid that actually ships is stacked core tiers + a short add-on menu, with a rule that add-ons cannot undercut a gated benefit.

### Cited Findings
- MLH: “When assigning perks to your tiers, we recommend having each tier build upon the level directly below it.” Lowest tier starts with basic perks in “general, publicity, and recruitment.” Custom packages “upon further negotiation,” noted at the bottom of the tier page. — [MLH Sponsorship Prospectus](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Instrumentl: “Benefits are cumulative, so each level includes everything below it plus something more visible.” Lower tiers lean on cheap digital/recognition; “save your scarce, high-value benefits, such as stage time, speaking opportunities, and exclusivity, for the top levels.” — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- SponsorFlo: price each asset a la carte first, then **bundle** so each tier is the sum of included assets; “each tier adds assets to the one below it”; each higher tier must include something the lower one **cannot buy** (naming, exclusivity). — [SponsorFlo: Sponsorship Pricing](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pricing-guide)
- Bizzabo (18 June 2026) lists three commercial models: (1) **tiered** Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum; (2) **bespoke a la carte** for enterprise buyers who refuse to pay for unused bundle items; (3) **thematic** packages (sustainability, thought leadership) built around a purpose rather than a visibility level. “If sponsors can’t immediately see why they’d step up… they’ll default to the lowest option every time.” — [Bizzabo: How To Craft Enticing Event Sponsorship Packages](https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/how-to-build-event-sponsorship-packages)
- i4a alternative: base fee for recognition + high-value items (session, lunch, happy hour) as add-ons. Works when needs vary widely and premium inventory is limited; “typically requires more consultative sales.” — [i4a 2026](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- Guidebook: à la carte = individual assets, more flexibility, often negotiated closer to the event; title = one exclusive top partnership. “Many successful events combine approaches”: tiers as foundation, à la carte add-ons for extra exposure. Allow “add-ons or upgrades” inside the tier system. — [Guidebook tiers](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- SponsorCX template (2026 worksheet): each tier has **fixed elements** (cannot be exchanged), **choice elements** (pick from a short list), and **add-ons** (separately priced, with delivery/approval rules). Example four-tier ladder: Entry $5k / Growth $12.5k / Strategic $30k / Flagship $75k+, with Flagship limited to 1 per category. — [SponsorCX package template PDF](https://www.sponsorcx.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Sponsorship-Tier-Package-Template.pdf)
- Cvent: inventory first (before / during / after the event), then packages around objectives; keep customization so the package “reflects the sponsor’s goals.” Product-side, Cvent even lets an exhibitor hold **multiple** sponsorship levels at once (2024 Exhibitor Packages). — [Cvent 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors); [Cvent Plan & Promote News, 19 June 2024](https://release.cvent.com/eventmanagement/announcements/plan-promote-news-for-19-june-2024)
- Eventbrite quotes JoJo Gutfarb: “Let sponsors know you can create something exclusively for them. If you don’t, you’re throwing money away.” Chris Baylis (Sponsorship Collective) sometimes **withholds prices** from brochures to force a conversation — a sales tactic, not a community-hackathon default. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- PCMA: 47% of planners pitch a **menu of options**; 22% craft customized approaches; many do both. Most popular menu items: speaking, receptions, physical branded items, networking. Hardest to sell: coffee breaks, dependent care, full meals, tech (Wi‑Fi, charging, app banners). — [PCMA Convene survey](https://www.pcma.org/convene-survey-event-sponsorships/)
- HubSpot offers a free proposal template with a **customizable tier table**, not a recommended economic model. — [HubSpot sponsorship proposal template](https://www.hubspot.com/resources/templates/sponsorship-proposal)
- Community-organizer playbook (Akilima / samber skills, not a trade-press study): price add-ons independently; a named branded surface may out-price a mid tier; an add-on **cannibalizes** the ladder if it resells a gated benefit (floor presence, stage time) for less — price the add-on above the cheapest tier that includes that benefit, or don’t sell it. — [Akilima: event-sponsor-pricing](https://www.akilima.tech/skills/samber/dev-event-organizer-skills/event-sponsor-pricing)

### Inferences
- For Abstracthon, **stacked 3-tier table + “custom packages available” footer** matches MLH and every small-event prospectus reviewed. A full a la carte menu is a large-conference sales motion (Bizzabo enterprise, PCMA 47% menu).
- Choice-within-tier (SponsorCX “choose one / choose two”) is useful when two sponsors at the same price want different activations (workshop vs. challenge vs. hiring table) without exploding SKUs.
- Do not sell “challenge + juror” as both a cheap add-on **and** a mid-tier perk: that is the cannibalization rule in the Akilima playbook and the reason stacked ladders exist.

### Gaps
- No public hackathon prospectus in this pass published a full à la carte rate card (logo $X, workshop $Y, juror $Z) alongside tiers; hackathons appear to negotiate customs off the stacked grid.
- Eventbrite’s “leave the price off” advice conflicts with MLH/hackathon practice of putting prices on a 2–3 page packet; no evidence which closes better for student events.

## Which benefits actually increase with price: logo size, speaking, booth, exclusivity, data, hiring, challenge/track ownership, jury, mentorship?

### Takeaway
Logos and listings scale cheaply and live in every tier; they do not justify the jump. What actually ladders is **scarce activation and rights**: stage minutes, booth/table, category exclusivity, opt-in data/hiring access, named challenge/track, a jury seat, and workshops. Visibility is the floor; participation rights are the product that sells Gold/Title.

### Cited Findings

**Visibility vs activation (the commercial distinction)**
- Guidebook: visibility = passive logo/banner; activation = interactive experiences. Activation “commands 2–3x higher rates than visibility-only options.” Measure impressions vs. actions (sign-ups, demos). — [Guidebook: Activation vs Visibility](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/sponsorship-activation-vs-visibility)
- Bizzabo CMO Alon Alroy (2026 State of Events): “Sponsors don’t want logo exposure alone. They want qualified conversations, co-created content, and real engagement data.” — [Bizzabo](https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/how-to-build-event-sponsorship-packages)
- i4a: sponsors prioritize leads, decision-maker access, thought leadership, brand awareness, market intelligence. **Low-value:** crowded logo pages, printed program ads, bag inserts, generic hallway banners. **Standard (all tiers):** website logo, program listing, social thank-you. **Medium:** booth, comps, featured email, strategic signage. **High (reserve for top):** speaking, exclusive session hosting, opt-in attendee list, private meeting room, reception hosting, opening/closing remarks, exclusive networking. — [i4a 2026](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- Cvent: sponsors buy “specific touchpoints,” not “a sponsorship in the abstract.” Inventory windows: before (web, email, social), during (stage, sessions, signage, named spaces, demos, lanyards, VIP, app), after (recap, co-branded content, post-event data). KPIs differ: awareness = impressions; lead-gen = meetings/lead quality; thought leadership = session attendance and content engagement. — [Cvent 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors)

**Logo size / placement (visibility that *does* ladder, weakly)**
- Guidebook: entry = website + print; mid = signage, badges, digital marketing; top = stage backdrops, keynote slides, app splash. “Size and placement matter.” — [Guidebook tiers](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- FIRST 2023: logo on **landing page** only from Gold up; Silver/Bronze/Exhibitor sit on the sponsor page. Company description word count: Diamond 500 → Exhibitor 50. Social posts: 4x / 4x / 2x / 1x. Ballroom backdrop yes for Bronze+; table signage during meals only Diamond–Gold. — [FIRST 2023](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
- Eventbrite conference template: Platinum/Gold on homepage + sponsor page; Silver/Bronze on sponsor page only; check-in logo starts at Silver; sponsored app emails 3x / 1x / 0 / 0. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- DevFest Guadalajara 2026: all three cash tiers get logo on site/social and on event identity; **banners/rollers in common areas, 30-min branded talk, stand, and talent-database access are Gold-only**. Gold limited to 3. Prices (MXN, ex-VAT): Oro $30,000 / Plata $20,000 / Bronce $12,000. — [DevFest GDL 2026 deck](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
- WTM + GDG Santa Cruz 2026 cash ladder (local 15 Oct 2026 letter; not Abstracthon): Plata Bs 1,500 / Oro Bs 2,000 / Platino Bs 3,000. Logo on web for all; social thank-you **group vs individual**; impact report general → detailed → personalized; VIP tickets 2 / 5 / 10; stage: none / mention in pauses / **5 minutes on the main event**; merch-in-kit and talent-networking from Oro; **stand only at Platino, preferential**. In-kind (catering, venue, swag, photo) is a **separate** track whose package depends on contribution value. — Workspace extract `research_notes/_pdf_extracts/_Carta_de_Sponsor.txt`; community hub [GDG Santa Cruz](https://gdg.community.dev/gdg-santa-cruz/)

**Speaking (gated, time-scaled)**
- Guidebook: “Reserve speaking slots for top tiers. This protects your content quality while creating genuine exclusivity.” — [Guidebook tiers](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- COMSOL Bengaluru 2023: presentation slot **Silver 10 min / Gold 20 min**; Bronze has none. Booth size also ladders (Silver 3×2 m, Gold 3×3 m). — [COMSOL prospectus](https://cdn.comsol.com/conference/2023/documents/pdf/COMSOL_Conference_SponsorProspectus_A4-Bengaluru.pdf)
- VTHacks 11: ceremony presentations 1 min opening (Bronze/Silver) vs 3 min opening (Gold) vs **5 min opening + 2 min closing (Diamond)**; tech talk 30 min vs 1 hour vs 1 hour. — [VTHacks 11 prospectus](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
- FIRST 2023 explicitly: “Sponsorship packages DO NOT include session speaking spots. All those interested in presenting… are required to submit through the Call for Speakers.” Diamond-only extras: 5-minute opening address with keynote introduction, 1-min video, joint press conference, podcast. — [FIRST 2023](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
- Instrumentl gala Presenting ($25k in their example): two-minute speaking moment + “presented by” co-branding. Gold gets verbal recognition from the stage, not a speaking slot. — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- Eventbrite: “you likely won’t offer a bottom-tier donor the opportunity to become a co-chair. This premium benefit is best reserved for top-tier donors.” — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)

**Booth / table (often a hard floor, not a Bronze perk)**
- AstonHack sample (UK): Gold and Silver get a sponsor table; **Bronze = “No on-site presence”**, merch only. Gold £1,500 / Silver £1,000 / Bronze £500. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- UK sample generic ladder: Low tier “No Workshop”; Mid 1–2 workshops; Top 2+ workshops; on-site presence starts mid/top. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- DevFest GDL 2026: stand is Gold-only. — [DevFest GDL deck](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
- WTM/GDG Santa Cruz: stand only at Platino. — Workspace extract `_Carta_de_Sponsor.txt`
- VTHacks: “Send reps + distribute swag” all tiers; **sponsor table** from Silver up (Bronze sends people but the table perk is marked starting Silver in the prospectus grid). Prize category, branded UX event, on-site interviews, “VTHacks presented by YOU” are Diamond. — [VTHacks 11](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
- i4a lists exhibit booth as a **medium** benefit for mid tiers, not a top-only asset — that is a large association trade-show assumption with a real exhibit hall. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- Akilima playbook: “floor presence starts at a hard tier, with no cheap route in beneath it”; also keep at least one **staff-free visibility tier** (logo only, no booth), because a booth costs the sponsor staff days and is the first line a tightening budget cuts. — [Akilima event-sponsor-pricing](https://www.akilima.tech/skills/samber/dev-event-organizer-skills/event-sponsor-pricing)

**Exclusivity**
- SponsorFlo: category exclusivity commonly **+30% to +50%** if enforceable on every asset; do not sell it if a competitor can still appear. Title example: (presenting assets + naming) × 1.3. — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pricing-guide)
- Anvara 2026 marketplace index: listed packages with exclusivity sit at **1.26×** the event’s median package (about +26%), and title/presenting at 2.00× without exclusivity vs 2.45× with it (~+22.5%). They warn this is listed-package data, “not a controlled estimate of exclusivity’s value or a universal surcharge.” — [Anvara Sponsorship Price Index 2026](https://www.anvara.com/blog/sponsorship-price-index-2026-event-costs-by-category)
- PCMA: 56% of surveyed planners offer category exclusivity for top-level sponsors. — [PCMA](https://www.pcma.org/convene-survey-event-sponsorships/)
- i4a Platinum/Title quantity **1–2**; Guidebook: cap highest tier at 1–3. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/); [Guidebook](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- SponsorCX: “If five sponsors all have ‘exclusive’ status in their category, none of them do.” — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)

**Data / hiring pipeline**
- i4a: opt-in attendee list is a **high-value, top-tier** item; privacy safeguards required. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- FIRST 2023 (large conference): even Exhibitors get limited **opt-in** pre/post lists; Diamond/Platinum get the pre-con list twice; **exclusive** pre- or post-con email to attendees is Diamond-only. — [FIRST 2023](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
- VTHacks: **resume book** from Gold up (early access at Diamond); **on-site interviews** Diamond. Social: story → non-solo post → solo post + Instagram takeover. — [VTHacks 11](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
- DevFest GDL: “acceso a base de talento” is bundled with Gold (stand + talk), not Plata/Bronce. — [DevFest GDL](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
- Bizzabo 2026 benchmark: programs deploy an average of **0.2 non-registration forms per event**, but those forms average **91 submissions** — structured lead capture is underused. — [Bizzabo](https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/how-to-build-event-sponsorship-packages)
- Large-conference caveat: badge scanning, LeadCapture licenses, attendee matchmaking are Cvent/Bizzabo products for trade shows, not a 50-person hackathon. — [Cvent 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors)

**Challenge / track ownership, jury, mentorship (hackathon-specific)**
- Gupta’s organizer guide (hackathon prizes/sponsors): four commercial products, not metals — **Title** (top billing, opening keynote, **first pick of judging seats**); **Track/Challenge** (named track, dedicated prize, **a judge on that track’s panel**); **Infrastructure** (in-kind credits → logo + welcome kit); **Community** (swag/food/venue → logo, maybe a table). Pitch: “a judging seat gives their engineers a reason to show up”; “a challenge track puts their API in front of builders.” — [Gupta: Hackathon Sponsorship & Prize Guide](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- AstonHack: Gold **and** Silver can “set a sponsor challenge” and sit on the **judging panel**; Gold adds workshops + extended opening presentations + top-level advertising. Bronze cannot set a challenge, cannot judge, cannot be on-site. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- UK generic sample: judging panel at mid and top, not low; workshops 0 / 1–2 / 2+. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- VTHacks Diamond: prize category + branded UX event + on-site interviews + “presented by YOU.” Lower tiers get smaller branding and shorter ceremony time; prize category is not on Bronze. — [VTHacks 11](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
- TreeHacks 2025 (large US collegiate; not a priced prospectus): sponsors “host workshops, discuss job opportunities, host prizes”; prize pages are **named sponsor challenges** (Best Use of X) judged to TreeHacks’ own criteria (creativity, technical complexity, social impact) plus sponsor-specific prizes. — [TreeHacks 2024 site](https://2024.treehacks.com/); [TreeHacks 2025 Devpost](https://treehacks-2025.devpost.com/)
- PennApps XXVI (2025): overall judging is organizer-owned (technology, design, completion, learning); sponsors appear as **named prize categories** (e.g. Bloomberg Sustainability, Capital One Finance) plus MLH tool tracks. That is challenge-as-prize, not challenge-as-event-governance. — [PennApps XXVI Devpost](https://pennapps-xxvi.devpost.com/)
- DocuSign as Gold sponsor at HackMIT (2019, older but explicit): company-run API challenges with **their** criteria, $1k/$500 prizes, judged by the sponsor team — parallel to, not replacing, the event’s overall judging. — [DocuSign: HackMIT 2019](https://www.docusign.com/blog/dsdev-hackmit-2019)

**Mentorship / workshops**
- UK/AstonHack: workshops are a **mid/top** perk; Bronze has none. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- Gupta timeline: 1 week out, “brief judges and mentors on each sponsor’s challenge criteria so scoring is consistent.” Mentors are an ops resource the organizer staffs; sponsor engineers showing up is a Title/Track activation, not a Bronze logo perk. — [Gupta](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- TreeHacks marketing copy: sponsors host workshops as part of partnership, not as a listed Bronze benefit (no public price grid found). — [TreeHacks 2024](https://2024.treehacks.com/)

### Inferences
- A practical ladder for a small hackathon, synthesized from the above (not Abstracthon prices):
  - **Low / visibility:** logo, grouped social, merch-in-kit if they ship it. No table, no jury, no named challenge, no talk.
  - **Mid / activation:** table or small stand, one workshop **or** one named prize/challenge, maybe one jury seat **for that prize**, grouped or short ceremony mention.
  - **High / ownership:** larger presence, longer stage time, hiring/resume or talent intro, first pick of challenge/track, jury seat (still one vote), maybe presenting language.
- Logo size is real but weak. If the only difference between Bs-equivalent Silver and Gold is a bigger PNG, buyers take Silver (Bizzabo’s “default to the lowest” warning).
- **Hiring** (resume book, interviews, talent DB) is consistently gated at Gold/Title in the public decks (VTHacks, DevFest GDL). Putting it in Bronze kills the hiring-sponsor upgrade.
- **Challenge + juror** is the hackathon analogue of “speaking + session hosting.” AstonHack sells it at **both** Gold and Silver; Gupta splits Title (first pick of seats) vs Track (one judge on that panel). Either works if Bronze cannot buy it.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 public prospectus was found that prices a **single jury seat** or **single challenge** as an à la carte line item.
- LATAM career-fair packages with resume-book pricing were not found in this pass.
- TreeHacks/HackMIT/PennApps sponsor **prices** are not public; only activation behavior is.

## How do events sell "more decision power" without promising the sponsor that they run the event?

### Takeaway
Sell a **bounded slice of the program** — a named prize, a track challenge, one jury seat, a time-boxed workshop — while the organizer keeps theme, eligibility, overall rubric, and winner certification. The contract language is “propose / designate / participate,” not “decide / control / chair the event.”

### Cited Findings
- Abstracthon internal draft already uses this pattern: sponsor may **propose a challenge on a track**; org “must coordinate with each sponsor the content of the challenge, delivery criteria, and publication,” and sponsors “designate a juror.” That is proposal + one designated judge, not event ownership. — Workspace extract `research_notes/_pdf_extracts/Abstracthon_Informacion_General.txt`
- Gupta: Title gets “first pick of judging seats”; Track/Challenge gets “a judge on **that track’s** panel” and a dedicated prize. Mentors/judges are briefed on **sponsor challenge criteria** so scoring is consistent — criteria for the sponsor prize, not a rewrite of the event. — [Gupta](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- AstonHack: Gold/Silver “set a sponsor challenge” and are “part of judging panel”; they do not become event directors. Opening-ceremony presentation length still ladders (extended only at Gold). — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- PennApps / TreeHacks: overall criteria remain organizer-owned; sponsors fund **parallel prize categories**. Teams can submit to both overall and sponsor prizes. — [PennApps XXVI](https://pennapps-xxvi.devpost.com/); [TreeHacks 2025 Devpost](https://treehacks-2025.devpost.com/)
- FIRST 2023 (large security conference): paid sponsorship **does not** buy a session on the agenda; speakers go through CFP. Diamond may give a 5-minute opening address — a ceremonial slot, not program control. — [FIRST 2023](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
- Instrumentl naming vocabulary: **Title** sponsor’s name appears before the event (“Acme presents…”); **Presenting** is featured prominently but “does not rename the event”; **Associate** shares billing. Year-round Signature Partner ($50k+ in their example) may get “a seat on an **advisory circle**” and a custom engagement plan — advisory, not board control. — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- Eventbrite gala template: top package includes naming rights for **one award**, not the gala itself; co-chair is reserved for top donors (gala fundraising language — not a hackathon role). — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- Cvent: structure the exclusive premium around “market leadership,” not operational control; measure thought-leadership sponsors on session attendance, not on whether they ran the agenda. — [Cvent 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors)
- Akilima playbook: a named live-captioning line is “visibility on the line, **never control of the program**.” Track/category exclusivity is a vendor/foundation-pole product with a hard cap; community-pole policy may forbid selling it. — [Akilima](https://www.akilima.tech/skills/samber/dev-event-organizer-skills/event-sponsor-pricing)
- SponsorCX: top tier wants “a sense of **ownership over part of the event**” and “something that other sponsors don’t get” — part, not the whole. Protect naming rights, keynote presenting, and exclusivity by tying them to a defined tier so they are not given away to close a cheap deal. — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)
- Guidebook hospitality perks (VIP, reserved seating, speaker meet-and-greet) “cost relatively little but feel high-value” and differentiate top tiers without transferring governance. — [Guidebook](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)

### Inferences
- Decision-power benefits that are safe to sell, with organizer veto intact:
  1. **Named prize / challenge on one track** (sponsor writes the prompt; org approves legality, fit, and fairness).
  2. **One juror** scoring that prize or sitting on a multi-judge panel (one vote, published rubric).
  3. **Workshop or tech talk** of fixed length, scheduled by the org.
  4. **Presenting language** (“track presented by”) without renaming Abstracthon.
  5. **First pick** among remaining challenge slots (Title), not exclusive ownership of all tracks.
- Decision-power benefits that imply they run the event (avoid, or reserve for a true Title with a written veto):
  - Event co-chair / ability to set overall theme, eligibility, or grand-prize winner.
  - Exclusive control of the jury.
  - Mandatory use of the sponsor’s product to be eligible for **overall** (as opposed to their own) prizes.
  - Agenda veto or the right to block other sponsors.
- Abstracthon already has four tracks (Finanzas, Educación, Comunicación, Ayuda tecnológica). Mapping “challenge owner” to **one track slice**, with org coordination, is the Gupta Track product — not Title.

### Gaps
- No hackathon contract template in this pass published the exact legal wording that separates “designate a juror” from “control judging.” Organizers should have counsel/Student Union review (UK Hackathons flags SU legal for contracts).
- No source quantified how many jury seats a 50–150 person hackathon can sell before judging legitimacy collapses.

## Typical add-ons: in-kind vs cash, a la carte extras, category exclusivity, title/presenting sponsor

### Takeaway
Keep a **cash ladder** as the public menu; put in-kind in a parallel “Official Supplier / Community Contributor” track valued at the **budget line it replaces**, not the donor’s retail sticker. Title/Presenting and category exclusivity are scarce add-ons or a capped top rung — never a Bronze checkbox. À la carte extras work if they do not resell a gated tier benefit cheaper.

### Cited Findings
- UK Hackathons: in-kind (prizes, venue, energy drinks) is common and useful to cut costs, “but remember that you do also need enough sponsors who can provide **cash**.” — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- Instrumentl: “Let lower tiers pay in-kind, not just cash” (caterer, printer) so small businesses can enter; in-kind fills a real budget line. — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- SponsorCX: create a **separate in-kind category** (Media Partner, Official Supplier, Community Contributor) outside the cash hierarchy; assign fair market value; define benefits; hybrid cash+in-kind is increasingly common. Custom major partners stay **outside** the standard tier so they don’t muddy the menu. — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)
- Guidebook: in-kind = products/services instead of cash; recognition varies with contribution value. Title sponsorship is a different model: single exclusive top partnership, usually 6–12 months out, all touchpoints. — [Guidebook](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- Gupta: Infrastructure (API credits, hosting) is an in-kind product with logo + kit mention; Community is swag/food/venue; Title is the largest cash/credit, usually **one per event**. — [Gupta](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- WTM/GDG Santa Cruz 2026 letter: section “Aporte en Servicios/Productos” lists catering, software discounts, rooms, décor, souvenirs, photo/video; “the corresponding package will depend on the value of the contribution.” Cash plans remain Plata/Oro/Platino. Invoice (factura) costs extra. All plans “open to negotiation.” — `_Carta_de_Sponsor.txt`
- Akilima: value in-kind at the **budget line it displaces**, never the donor’s stated retail; that displaced-cost value is what buys tier placement. Negotiation policy should be written before the first call: negotiable = payment schedule, equal-value swaps, bundling add-ons; **never negotiable** = published tier prices and anything program policy forbids. — [Akilima](https://www.akilima.tech/skills/samber/dev-event-organizer-skills/event-sponsor-pricing)
- SponsorCX community-sports example: 3 cash tiers + add-ons sold separately (Awards Ceremony Presenting $3,000; Kids Fun Run $2,000; Water Station $500 each). Keeps the ladder clean while capturing extra revenue. — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)
- i4a à la carte: session, lunch, happy hour as add-ons on top of a base recognition fee. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- PCMA: popular à la carte: speaking, receptions, lanyards/keycards/signage, networking. Unpopular: coffee, dependent care, full meals, Wi‑Fi/charging/app banners. 56% sell category exclusivity at the top. — [PCMA](https://www.pcma.org/convene-survey-event-sponsorships/)
- Category exclusivity pricing: SponsorFlo +30–50% if enforceable; Anvara listed-package premium ~26% (not a universal surcharge). Title/presenting packages in Anvara data sit at **2.00×** median without exclusivity and **2.45×** with it. — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pricing-guide); [Anvara 2026](https://www.anvara.com/blog/sponsorship-price-index-2026-event-costs-by-category)
- Instrumentl: Title = name before the event; Presenting = featured, does not rename; many orgs add Presenting/Title **above** Platinum. Gala example: Presenting $25k includes co-branding + main-stage banner + two-minute speech. — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- Eventbrite 10k template: Event Partner $5k gets awards-presentation rights (a presenting-like perk) vs Race Sponsor $2.5k without it. Gala top tier names **one award**, not the event. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- VTHacks Diamond: “VTHacks presented by YOU” — naming is the top-rung exclusive, quantity implied scarce. — [VTHacks 11](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
- DevFest GDL: no separate Title; instead Gold is capped at 3 and holds the only talk/stand/talent-DB bundle. — [DevFest GDL](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
- Discounts as add-on policy: i4a early-commitment 10–15% for 4–6 months out; SponsorFlo early-bird 15–25% (6+ months) and multi-year 10–20%/year; COMSOL offered 10% early bird. Discount for **commitment**, not to close a hesitant deal (SponsorFlo). — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/); [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pricing-guide); [COMSOL](https://cdn.comsol.com/conference/2023/documents/pdf/COMSOL_Conference_SponsorProspectus_A4-Bengaluru.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Bolivia student hackathon, two parallel doors: **cash tiers** (public grid) and **in-kind** (food, venue, swag, API credits, volunteer prizes) mapped onto a tier by displaced budget, with a cash floor if they also want a jury/challenge.
- Title/Presenting should be **one logo in the event name or “presented by”** — and only if a buyer exists. If not, cap Gold at 1–3 (DevFest GDL pattern) instead of inventing an empty Title.
- Category exclusivity (e.g. only one bank on Finanzas) is meaningful **if** Abstracthon will actually refuse a second bank. If the org will take every check, do not sell exclusivity.
- Volunteer-prize in-kind (the Abstracthon draft’s alternative to Bs 500) is a Community/Infrastructure product in Gupta’s taxonomy — visibility, not challenge ownership — unless the in-kind value matches a higher cash tier.

### Gaps
- No public LATAM hackathon contract was found that states how in-kind is converted into a named metal tier.
- HubSpot’s template does not discuss in-kind, exclusivity, or Title; it is layout-only.
- Anvara vs SponsorFlo exclusivity premiums conflict (≈26% marketplace vs 30–50% “common practice”); treat as a range, not a formula.

## Pricing heuristics for small vs large events (percent of budget, round numbers, anchor the top tier)

### Takeaway
Set the **top price first** as an ambitious-but-sellable anchor, then step down in 2–4× ratios, using round numbers sponsors can approve. Percent-of-budget rules from hackathon guides conflict with each other; use them as a sanity check, not a formula. Copy **ratios and scarcity**, not US/MXN/GBP sticker prices, and do not invent Abstracthon Bs figures.

### Cited Findings
- MLH: three tiers; “Your most expensive tier should **not be more than 25% of your budget**.” Prospectus 2–3 pages; first email should not even attach the deck (phishing filters). — [MLH](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- UK Hackathons sample **conflicts with MLH on the top share**: Top ~**30–40%** of hackathon budget, Mid ~15–25%, Low ~10%. AstonHack worked example: £1,500 / £1,000 / £500 (top = 3× bottom, steps of £500). First-time mix: more low-tier volume than high. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- Eventbrite: if the event needs $100,000, set a sponsorship goal of **$50,000 or higher**; top tier “around $15,000 to $20,000.” (This is a large-event fundraising heuristic, not a hackathon rule.) 80% of organizers in Eventbrite’s 2023 Event Trends Report cited budget cuts as a hurdle. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- Eventbreakeven 2026 guide (fetch of the full page failed with HTTP 500; claims below are from the search extract of that URL): Title/Presenting (1 available) priced at 100% of a per-attendee calculation as the **anchor**; Gold 50–60% of title (2–3 available); Silver 25–35% (3–5); Bronze 10–15% (unlimited, “mostly to give small local businesses a way to participate”). — [Eventbreakeven: How to Price Event Sponsorship Packages (2026)](https://eventbreakeven.com/blog/event-sponsorship-pricing)
- i4a: **anchor to the top tier**; top should be about **3–4× the bottom**, not 10×; extreme jumps make the top look unreachable and cluster buyers at the bottom. Example of compressed vs stretched: $3k/$5k/$8k/$15k vs $2.5k/$5k/$10k/$25k. Watch **approval thresholds** (e.g. $5k / $10k sign-off). Early-bird 10–15%; annual increases 5–10%. Fulfillment cost must not eat the package (don’t let a $5k package cost $3k to deliver). Real scarcity only (finite session slots), not fake caps. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- SponsorFlo: 3–5 tiers; **top ≈ 2–3× the middle**; new program with no data: cost recovery × **1.5–2.0** markup, then switch to CPM/value. Worked new-program example: ($15,000 ÷ 10 sponsors) × 1.5 = $2,250 each (flat, then differentiate). Round-number examples they themselves use: $10k / $25k / $50k. “Pricing low to get started… sets a baseline that’s hard to raise.” — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pricing-guide)
- SponsorCX: “It’s tempting to price tiers at $5,000, $10,000, and $25,000 because those numbers feel right. But pricing should reflect actual value… not just what sounds reasonable.” Still, published decks overwhelmingly use round numbers. — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)
- Instrumentl: start from revenue goal; highest tier “just below the reach gift you’d be thrilled to receive”; entry tier a local small business can approve **without a committee**; add one **reach** tier above what you expect anyone to take, to reframe the middle. — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- Guidebook: “Price based on value, not cost. A speaking slot costs nothing but delivers significant value.” Underpricing top packages “leaves money on the table.” — [Guidebook](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- PCMA: ~39% of planners say pricing is **value-based**; 25% **expense recovery**; sponsors = 28% of event revenue / 18% of org revenue **at large professional events**. — [PCMA](https://www.pcma.org/convene-survey-event-sponsorships/)
- Akilima (community-conference skill, not a survey): sponsorship share **75–80%+** of budget at community-conference scale; **~100%** for a free hackathon with no ticket line. That total is what the whole card must raise — “never a number to split evenly across tiers.” Present top first; make the **middle** the visibly best value. — [Akilima](https://www.akilima.tech/skills/samber/dev-event-organizer-skills/event-sponsor-pricing)
- Cvent: $97.4 billion in corporate sponsorships in 2022 (macro context, not a local rate). Outreach minimum ~6 months; Q4 is when many marketing budgets lock. Proposal 4–5 pages with a 1-pager up front. First-time events have less proof, so use community demographics and comparable events. — [Cvent 2026](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/how-to-get-event-sponsors)
- Published small/community prices (comparables, **not** Abstracthon):
  - AstonHack: £500 / £1,000 / £1,500. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
  - WTM/GDG Santa Cruz 2026 (same city ecosystem): Bs 1,500 / 2,000 / 3,000 for year-event cash plans; top = 2× entry. — `_Carta_de_Sponsor.txt`
  - DevFest Guadalajara 2026: MXN 12,000 / 20,000 / 30,000; top = 2.5× entry; Gold capped at 3. — [DevFest GDL](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
  - VTHacks 11 (large US university MLH event, 500+ registrants claimed): $3,000 / $6,000 / $8,000 / $11,000; Diamond ≈ 3.7× Bronze, only 1.4× Gold — a compressed top. — [VTHacks 11](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
  - COMSOL Bengaluru 2023 (~200 specialists): ₹75,000 / ₹90,000 / ₹125,000 (tight 1.7× from Bronze to Gold). — [COMSOL](https://cdn.comsol.com/conference/2023/documents/pdf/COMSOL_Conference_SponsorProspectus_A4-Bengaluru.pdf)
  - FIRST 2023 (large international conference): $6k–$45k. — [FIRST](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
  - i4a association ranges (US, large): Bronze $1k–$5k through Platinum $15k–$50k+. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- Abstracthon internal budget **references** (not sponsor prices): food tope ~Bs 1,500 for 50 people; economic prizes Bs 200; raffle fund Bs 130; suggested **flat** sponsor ask Bs 500 or a volunteer prize. — `Abstracthon_Informacion_General.txt`

### Inferences
- **Small community / first hackathon:** round local currency, 3 rungs, top about 2–3× entry (WTM SC, DevFest GDL, AstonHack). Anchor the conversation on the top package even if most signatures land in the middle (i4a, Eventbreakeven, Akilima).
- **Large conference:** 4+ rungs, dollar five-figures, data products, exhibit halls (FIRST, i4a, PCMA). Do not import those stickers to Santa Cruz/Oruro/Cochabamba.
- **Percent-of-budget:** treat MLH’s “top tier ≤ 25% of budget” and UK’s “top 30–40%” as a **conflict**. Sanity check: if one sponsor would fund almost the whole event, the top is too high *or* the budget is too small to support a Title story. If the top is 10× the bottom (i4a warning), the top will not sell.
- **Round numbers win in the wild** despite SponsorCX’s warning; i4a’s approval-threshold point is the better reason to pick 1,500 / 3,000 rather than 1,740.
- Abstracthon’s flat Bs 500 sits at AstonHack **Bronze** and **below** WTM Santa Cruz Plata (Bs 1,500) — as an *entry* visibility price it is in-family for a tiny event; as the price of **challenge + juror** it is below every public ladder that gates those rights. That is a design issue, not a recommended Abstracthon price.

### Gaps
- Eventbreakeven full article could not be fetched (HTTP 500); per-attendee formula details beyond the search extract are unverified.
- No public 2024–2026 Bolivia hackathon prospectus with three priced tiers was found besides the WTM/GDG community letter (which is for a year of community events, not a single hackathon).
- Exchange-rate and purchasing-power conversion of US/UK/MXN decks into Bs is out of scope; organizers must set local numbers.
- Cvent’s $97.4B (2022) is global sponsorship spend, useless as a local heuristic.

## What should NEVER be in a low tier if you want the high tier to sell?

### Takeaway
Keep Bronze/entry as **cheap-to-fulfill visibility**. If the entry package already includes a table, a talk, a named challenge, a juror, hiring data, or “presented by,” there is no reason to climb. Those are the scarce rights that create the value gap Bizzabo and i4a say you must make “impossible to miss.”

### Cited Findings
- Bizzabo: if the value gap is not obvious, sponsors “default to the lowest option every time.” — [Bizzabo](https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/how-to-build-event-sponsorship-packages)
- Guidebook mistakes list: creating too many tiers; promising vague benefits (“premium visibility”); overselling exclusivity; putting speaking in the wrong place. Explicitly: reserve speaking for top; entry = logo + program. — [Guidebook](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/what-are-sponsorship-tiers)
- i4a **do not put in Bronze** (their “high-value / reserve for top” list): dedicated speaking or panel; exclusive session hosting; attendee list; private meeting room; reception/meal hosting; opening/closing remarks; exclusive VIP networking. Standard-only for all tiers: website logo, program, social thank-you, listing. — [i4a](https://www.i4a.com/blog/event-sponsorship-packages/)
- Instrumentl: “Save your scarce, high-value benefits, such as stage time, speaking opportunities, and exclusivity, for the top levels.” Presenting adds co-branding, main-stage banner, speaking moment. — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/how-to-create-nonprofit-sponsorship-levels)
- Eventbrite: do not give bottom-tier donors **co-chair**; arrange gifts by tier. Their Bronze conference example is logo on sponsor page + comps + F&B — no homepage, no check-in branding, no sponsored emails. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/sponsorship-levels-ds00/)
- SponsorCX: tying naming rights, keynote presenting, and exclusivity to a defined tier is how you avoid “giving them away at lower price points just to close a deal.” Do not ignore lower tiers operationally, but do not load them with flagship rights. — [SponsorCX](https://www.sponsorcx.com/sponsorship-tiers-gold-silver-bronze-and-beyond/)
- AstonHack: Bronze has **no on-site presence, no challenge, no judging panel, no workshop**. Those four are exactly what Silver/Gold sell. — [UK Hackathons](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- DevFest GDL: Bronce/Plata do **not** get rollers in common areas, the 30-min talk, the stand, or the talent database. — [DevFest GDL](https://brochure.gdgguadalajara.com/DevFest%20Guadalajara%202026%20-%20Sponsor%20Deck.pdf)
- WTM/GDG Santa Cruz: Plata has **no** pause mention, no merch-in-kit, no talent-networking, no stand; 5-minute main-stage slot is Platino-only. — `_Carta_de_Sponsor.txt`
- VTHacks: Bronze does not get prize category, “presented by,” on-site interviews, Instagram takeover, large banners, or the long ceremony slot. — [VTHacks 11](https://2023.vthacks.com/prospectus.pdf)
- FIRST: exclusive attendee email, opening address, press conference, podcast, private meeting room, social-event guest tickets are Diamond (or Diamond/Platinum) — not Bronze. — [FIRST 2023](https://www.first.org/conference/2023/sponsorship-details)
- Guidebook activation vs visibility: entry-level = logo + program mentions; premium adds exclusive activations (sponsored sessions, branded lounges). Underpricing activation is a listed mistake. — [Guidebook activation](https://www.guidebook.com/glossary/sponsorship-activation-vs-visibility)
- Akilima: no cheap route to **floor presence** beneath the booth-floor tier; do not sell add-ons that recreate a gated benefit for less. — [Akilima](https://www.akilima.tech/skills/samber/dev-event-organizer-skills/event-sponsor-pricing)

### Inferences
- A “never in the low tier” checklist for a small tech/hackathon event, synthesized from the cited decks (adapt; do not copy prices):
  1. Event naming / “presented by” / title lockup
  2. Category exclusivity (only bank, only cloud, only telco)
  3. Opening/closing remarks or any untimed main-stage speech
  4. Named challenge / track ownership
  5. Jury designation (unless the org *wants* challenge+juror to be the mid-tier hook — then it still must not be in the lowest rung)
  6. Workshop / tech talk
  7. Booth/table (keep a staff-free logo tier)
  8. Resume book, opt-in attendee list, on-site interviews, “talent database”
  9. Solo social takeover / individual campaign (grouped thank-you is fine)
  10. Co-chair, advisory control, or veto over other sponsors
- Abstracthon’s internal flat package currently puts **challenge + juror** (high-ladder rights) at the same ask as a volunteer gift (Community/in-kind). Research models would split that: visibility/in-kind at entry; challenge+juror at mid or high, with org coordination retained.

### Gaps
- No source states a universal rule that “jury must never be in Silver”; AstonHack sells jury at Silver **and** Gold, but never Bronze. The hard rule is: **not in the lowest paid rung** if a higher rung exists.
- No experimental evidence on how many flagship perks you can leak into Bronze before Gold stop selling — only repeated practitioner warnings.
