# Anatomy of a sponsor call deck / sponsorship prospectus / partnership deck

Context for all inferences below: Abstracthon 2026 is a small community/campus hackathon in Bolivia organized with Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors. Advice written for 5,000-person B2B conferences (Cvent-style 12-page packets, EventIQ measurement contracts, Who's In 8–10 month corporate budget cycles) is flagged as such. The closest analog class is university/community hackathon prospectuses (MLH, Hacktech) plus small-event one-pagers, with a Spanish *carta de presentación* as the outreach vehicle.

## What is the usual document format and length for a sponsor call (deck vs prospectus vs one-pager vs carta)?

### Takeaway
These are not one document. A short PDF prospectus (hackathons: 2–3 pages; conferences: 5–12 pages) is the reusable sales packet; a 5–15 slide visual deck is for live meetings or cold outreach; a one-pager generates interest; a *carta*/email opens the door and should not carry the full packet on first contact. For a campus/community hackathon, the working set is: carta → 2–3 page PDF prospectus with 3 priced tiers → optional 5–10 slide deck for a call.

### Cited Findings
- Eventbrite defines a sponsorship prospectus as “a comprehensive guide to your event and the sponsorship opportunities that exist around it,” used as a leave-behind when the sponsor cannot take a call, as a PDF for internal Slack/#sponsorship forwarding, and as a slide deck in pitch meetings. A one-pager “may work for a small, community event” while a “world-famous festival requires something more comprehensive.” — [Eventbrite, How to Write a Sponsorship Prospectus](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/academy/sample-prospectus-how-to-write-a-winning-event-sponsorship-prospectus/)
- Sponsorship Collective (Jun 2026) separates four artifacts by sales stage: one-pager (generate interest / initial outreach), deck (educate and engage / sponsor discussions), proposal (custom recommendations after discovery), package (internal inventory, not the best sponsor-facing close document). “A company that has never heard of your organization does not need a detailed proposal.” — [Sponsorship Collective, Deck vs One-Pager vs Proposal](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/blog/sponsorship-deck-vs-one-pager-vs-proposal/)
- Visme (May 2025) distinguishes proposal vs deck vs letter: send a sponsorship request letter first; after interest, share the proposal and book a meeting; in the meeting, present a more visual deck. Their event proposal template is 10 US-Letter pages (PDF); their interactive event-sponsorship presentation template is 16 slides (PDF/PPTX/HTML5). Pro tip: also include “a one-pager they can print or forward with key takeaways, pricing, and contact info.” — [Visme, Sponsorship Proposal Templates](https://visme.co/blog/sponsorship-proposal-template/); [Visme event proposal template specs](https://www.visme.co/templates/proposals/event-sponsorship-proposal-example-1425294741/); [Visme interactive presentation specs](https://www.visme.co/templates/presentations/event-sponsorship-interactive-presentation-templates-1425285733/)
- MLH (current organizer guide): prospectus/packet should be **2–3 pages**. “Sponsors generally don't have time to read through a big sponsorship deck.” First email must **not** contain links, attachments, images, or the prospectus (IT phishing filters); send the packet 1–2 weeks later. — [MLH Sponsorship Prospectus](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- MLH Hackcon “Sponsorship 101” workshop (Jon Mark Go): three document types — (1) Standard Tiers Document, **usually 1–2 pages**, best when sponsor is ready to buy; (2) PowerPoint Deck, **usually 5–10 slides**, “Best For: Cold Emailing”; (3) Custom Proposals, **usually 1–5 pages**, for non-fixed pricing and in-kind. Always include event name, date, location, projected attendance, website URL, contact email/phone. — [MLH Hackcon IV workshop slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mlh-hackcon-iv-hackathon-sponsorship-101-workshop/63544317)
- CHARGE (sponsorship consultants): a “sponsorship deck” is **not** a formal proposal with locked pricing; “Its only job is to help convince a prospect to schedule a meeting.” Formal fees belong in the 1:1 or a later proposal. Eight-section structure. — [CHARGE, How to Successfully Build a Sponsorship Deck](https://chargesponsorship.com/how-to-successfully-build-a-sponsorship-deck-2/)
- Eventbrite pitch-deck guide (Dec 2022): convert the deck to **PDF** (do not send a large PPTX); or send a Drive/Dropbox/website link. Keep materials concise because sponsors receive many requests. — [Eventbrite, Create the Perfect Event Sponsorship Pitch Deck](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/ds00-perfecting-your-sponsorship-pitch-deck/)
- ConferenceDatabase (Sep 2025): a “powerful proposal usually lands somewhere between **5 and 10 pages**”; send as PDF with reasonable file size. — [ConferenceDatabase, Event Sponsorship Proposal Template](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- Who's In (event-tech vendor, community/conference template): “Keep the whole document **under 12 pages**.” Five sections: event overview, tiers, ROI metrics, testimonials, one-page booking form. Send **8–10 months** before the event (corporate budgets); “Never send a prospectus less than 4 months before.” — [Who's In, Sponsor Prospectus Template](https://whos-in.app/resources/sponsor-prospectus-template)
- Cvent (Feb 2020, older than the 2022–2026 preference window but still the most-cited page-by-page outline): 12+ pages including cover, TOC, objective, team, levels, representation, images, social proof, deadline, T&Cs, feedback, signature. This is a large-conference packet, not a campus one-pager. — [Cvent, Event Sponsorship Proposal Template](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- EventIQ (Sep 2026): prospectus is “a sales document and a measurement agreement at the same time” with **12 sourced sections**. Written for associations/AMCs/B2B organizers selling to finance teams. “Order matters more than length.” — [EventIQ, Sponsorship Prospectus Template](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Xarify (Dec 2025): “proposal deck should be **no more than 10 slides**. Each slide should carry one idea.” Cites Event Marketer on a shift to “deck-first, document-second.” A 12-page Word wall of text is “a liability.” — [Xarify, Sponsorship Pitch Mistakes](https://xarify.com/blog/sponsorship-pitch-mistakes-lose-deal-30-seconds.html)
- DealDuck (Mar 2026, vendor blog): “12–18 slides max”; claims “the average sponsorship deck gets about 11 seconds of attention” (unsourced in the article). Design so the PDF stands alone when forwarded internally. — [DealDuck, How to Build a Sponsorship Deck That Doesn't Get Ignored](https://dealduck.ai/blog/sponsorship-deck-guide/)
- SponsorFlo: eight foundation slides; “eight to twelve slides is enough.” — [SponsorFlo, Sponsorship Pitch Deck Templates](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pitch-deck-templates)
- AnyGen/Visme recap (citing Visme Mar 2026 and Eventbrite Mar 2025): “usually 10 to 15 slides.” — [AnyGen, Event Sponsorship Deck Template](https://www.anygen.io/showcase/event-sponsorship-deck-template/index.html)
- HubSpot free template: Word or PDF; page 1 = event + sponsor benefit + the ask; then logistics; then a customizable tier table; conclusion = benefits + next steps. — [HubSpot, Sponsorship Proposal Template](https://www.hubspot.com/resources/templates/sponsorship-proposal)
- EventsCase (ES, Jul 2025): the *informe de patrocinio* is the structured packet; it is **accompanied by** a *carta de presentación* that is concise and professional. The carta is not a substitute for the informe. — [EventsCase, Cómo pedir patrocinio para un evento](https://eventscase.es/blog/como-pedir-patrocinio-para-un-evento-guia)
- Bizzabo 2024 handbook includes a **sponsorship request letter** template (email subject “Partnership Opportunity: Sponsorship for [Event name]”) that attaches “our comprehensive sponsorship package.” — [Bizzabo, The Strategic Event Sponsorship Handbook (2024 PDF)](https://insights.si/wp-content/uploads/bizzabo-2024.-the-strategic-event-sponsorhip-handbook.pdf)
- Indiana University community-arts supplement (2023): typical formats PDF or PNG; “Consider sending a condensed, one-page version to attract interest and follow up with a longer, more detailed version tailored to the sponsor.” — [Vasconi, Corporate Sponsorship Prospectus (IUScholarWorks, 2023)](https://hdl.handle.net/2022/29523)
- Public hackathon packets: Hacktech 2025 (Caltech, 36-hour campus hackathon) is a short PDF “Sponsor Guide” opening with a welcome letter from the executive directors, then a branding/recruiting/benefits matrix with Platinum $10K / Gold $5K / Silver $2K / Bronze $1K, plus “ALL DETAILS ARE NEGOTIABLE.” Contact: hacktech@caltech.edu. — [Hacktech 2025 Sponsor Guide](https://www.hack.caltech.edu/2025/2025sponsor.pdf)
- AAAI Hackathon 2025 is a short package PDF: what the event is, who attends, four why-sponsor bullets (brand, recruitment, innovation leadership, networking), then Platinum $15k / Gold $10k / Silver $5k / Bronze $2.5k, then named contacts and an invitation to tailor / in-kind. — [AAAI Hackathon Sponsorship 2025 PDF](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Hackathon-Sponsorship-2025.pdf)
- Mosaic Lowell 2024 community-festival template: short packet with TOC (About Us, Introduction, Goals, Sponsorship Opportunities, Highlights, Acknowledgements) plus contact block. Uses prior-year numbers and next-year estimated goals. — [Mosaic Lowell TEMPLATE_Event Sponsorship Packet (May 2024)](https://mosaiclowell.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/TEMPLATE_Event-Sponsorship-Packet.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Abstracthon 2026, treat “deck de llamado” as a **2–3 page PDF prospectus** (MLH/Hacktech class), not a 12–18 slide conference sales deck and not a Cvent 12-page legal packet. Pair it with a ½-page *carta* and, if a meeting happens, a 5–10 slide visual deck.
- PPTX as an email attachment is discouraged (Eventbrite). PDF is the leave-behind because it forwards internally without the sender in the room (DealDuck, Eventbrite, ConferenceDatabase).
- “Carta” in Spanish-language practice is the **cover letter/email**, not the prospectus. EventsCase: who you are, what you want, why this company, what you propose, next step. Bizzabo’s letter template is the English equivalent and **attaches** the package rather than embedding it.
- Pricing-in-first-touch is a real split (see question 2 and 6). Hackathon practice (MLH, Hacktech, AAAI) **does** put prices in the short prospectus. CHARGE/Xarify withhold price on a *cold intro deck* whose only job is a meeting. For a small Bolivian community event selling to local companies, include prices in the prospectus (sponsors will not take a discovery call just to learn the number).
- Who's In “never send less than 4 months out / 8–10 months for corporates” assumes annual marketing budgets at 5,000-person conferences. Local SMEs and campus-adjacent brands in Bolivia often decide on a shorter cycle; still start early for any multinational (Microsoft ecosystem, banks, telcos).

### Gaps
- No current Splash (Splashthat) sponsorship-prospectus anatomy guide was found in 2022–2026 search results; Splash is frequently named in practitioner lists but the public docs located were Eventbrite, Cvent, Bizzabo, Webex, HubSpot, Visme.
- Bizzabo’s older “How to Write an Effective Virtual Event Sponsorship Proposal” URL returned 404; the 2024 handbook and the older HubSpot-hosted *Event Sponsorship Guide* PDF remain. — attempted [Bizzabo virtual proposals](https://www.bizzabo.com/blog/virtual-event-sponsorship-proposals)
- DealDuck’s “11 seconds of attention” and Xarify’s “Event Marketer industry reporting” on deck-first formats are vendor claims without a retrievable primary study in this pass.
- No Bolivia-specific or Spanish-LATAM hackathon prospectus template was found; EventsCase (Spain) is the closest Spanish-language event-industry source.

## What is the recommended slide/section order, and why?

### Takeaway
Nearly every serious source runs the same narrative, even when labels differ: **fit/opportunity → event in one breath → audience (the product) → proof → offer/activations → priced packages → how to buy**. Audience and proof come *before* the price table because the price is supposed to rest on evidence. Organizer biography, TOC, and legal terms are late or optional; they are not the story.

### Cited Findings
- Webex Events 2023 guide, “6 elements to include when creating an event sponsorship proposal,” in this order: (1) high-level event specifics, (2) value proposition, (3) social proof, (4) package and benefits details including price, (5) data about similar past events / audience, (6) event software/measurement stack. Element 1 exists “to immediately gauge whether or not this opportunity will be a good fit.” Element 2 is “Here’s why you should partner with my business to sponsor this event.” — [Webex Events, The 2023 Guide to Event Sponsorship (PDF)](https://www.webex.com/content/dam/www/us/en/documents/products/pdf/suite/events/webex-events-guide-event-sponsorship-2023.pdf)
- CHARGE 8-slide order and jobs: (1) Background — why *this* sponsor; (2) About Us — sponsorship-ready + brand fit; (3) Audience — “the most critical part,” “the product you’re selling”; (4) Marketing reach (social, digital, event, PR with specific figures); (5) Opportunity / menu of assets tied to ROI; (6) Unique activation idea; (7) Investment (omit on first outreach); (8) Thank-you + specific next step. Test for every section: “Is this here for me, or is this here for the sponsor?” — [CHARGE](https://chargesponsorship.com/how-to-successfully-build-a-sponsorship-deck-2/)
- DealDuck recommended structure (12–15 slides): Hook → Opportunity/gap → Audience, *their* way → Experience (story, not inventory list) → Social proof (3–4 partner stories) → Packages → Activation ideas for *this* brand → Let’s talk. — [DealDuck](https://dealduck.ai/blog/sponsorship-deck-guide/)
- EventIQ: 12 sections in this order, with rationale: Event and edition → Audience composition → Attendance history → Package contents by level → Price and what it is built from → What you will count → What you will not count → Shortfall terms → Data/consent → Reporting date/format → Last edition’s delivery → Deadlines and contact. “Audience composition and attendance history come before packages and prices, because they are the evidence a price rests on.” Opening-with-mission is listed as the first common mistake. — [EventIQ](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Eventbrite prospectus fundamentals, in listed order: Event Overview → Social Proof → Audience Breakdown → Sponsorship Details (upsides first, then options). Overview should explain format, concept, who it’s for, cadence, history — “Avoid straying too far into logistics.” — [Eventbrite prospectus](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/academy/sample-prospectus-how-to-write-a-winning-event-sponsorship-prospectus/)
- ConferenceDatabase “Essential Elements”: Executive Summary → Audience Demographics → Sponsorship Tiers/Packages → Activation Opportunities → Measurement & Reporting. “The most compelling part of your proposal isn't about how great your conference is—it’s about the audience.” — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- Cvent page order (large-conference packet, 2020): 1 Introduction/cover (with *sponsor’s* name and date) → 2 TOC → 3 Objective (why it matters + sponsor value) → 4 Meet the Team → 5 Sponsorship Levels (tables, not paragraphs) → 6 How Sponsors Will Be Represented (before/during/after) → 7 Images + past-sponsor logos + attendee infographic → 8 Social Proof → 9 Deadline → 10+ T&Cs → 11+ Feedback → 12+ Signature & thank you. — [Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- Visme typical proposal contents: Cover → TOC → Introduction → Event Details → Audience slide → Sponsorship levels → Marketing plan → Budget breakdown → Event timeline → CTA. Writing sequence: “introduce your cause with strategic storytelling” then specifics, audience, 2–3 levels, marketing plan, budget, timeline, CTA. — [Visme](https://visme.co/blog/sponsorship-proposal-template/)
- HubSpot: first page = event + how the sponsor benefits + what you’re asking; next = when/where/who/outcomes; then tier table; conclusion = benefits + next steps. — [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/resources/templates/sponsorship-proposal)
- Who's In five-section prospectus: (1) event overview — audience size, demographics, format; (2) tier structure; (3) ROI metrics; (4) testimonials; (5) one-page booking form. — [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/resources/sponsor-prospectus-template)
- EventsCase *informe* order: Introducción (nombre, fechas, objetivos, temática, histórico) → Perfil del público → Opciones de patrocinio (benefits, inversión, cupos/exclusividades) → KPIs → Casos de éxito. Carta: quién eres, qué buscas, por qué ellos, qué propones, siguiente paso. — [EventsCase](https://eventscase.es/blog/como-pedir-patrocinio-para-un-evento-guia)
- SponsorFlo 8 slides: Cover (both logos) → Why this brand → Who you are (short) → Audience with measurement source → Packages with prices → Mockup of their logo on the asset → Proof → Next step. “Who you are” is slide 3, not 1. — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pitch-deck-templates)
- AnyGen 12-slide event structure: Cover → Event snapshot → Why this event matters → Audience profile → Reach and promotion → Past results → Opportunities → Packages → Benefits matrix → Fulfillment timeline → Reporting plan → Contact and CTA. Rule: if a slide does not help answer audience fit, value, proof, price, or next step, remove it. — [AnyGen](https://www.anygen.io/showcase/event-sponsorship-deck-template/index.html)
- MLH prospectus contents (not a slide-by-slide list, but required blocks): basics on every page (name, date, projected attendance, URL, contact) → story (team, what you’re trying to accomplish, **what makes the event unique**) → three cumulative tiers with prices + “custom packages available” at the bottom of the tier page. Lowest tier = general + publicity + recruitment perks. Most expensive tier ≤ 25% of budget. — [MLH](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Hacktech 2025 actual order: welcome letter (mission + how funds are used + flexibility) → branding matrix (logo size, swag, blurb, banner) → recruiting matrix (materials, resumes, recruiters, onsite interviews) → additional benefits (booth, theme/prize/workshop, exclusive Caltech hour, judges, keynote) → explanatory blurbs → contact. — [Hacktech 2025 PDF](https://www.hack.caltech.edu/2025/2025sponsor.pdf)
- AAAI 2025 actual order: what the hackathon is → who attends → four sponsor-outcome bullets → dates/format → Platinum/Gold/Silver/Bronze with perks → named chair + aaai25@aaai.org → in-kind/custom invitation. — [AAAI 2025 PDF](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Hackathon-Sponsorship-2025.pdf)
- Partnership-deck transfer (not investor decks): senior leaders evaluate in this order — hypothesis of what we’d do together → each side’s contribution → quantified mutual upside → operating model → low-risk pilot → the ask today. Leading with company background is Mistake 1. — [Hazeldine, The Partnership Pitch (6-slide)](https://medium.com/@marybeth.hazeldine/the-partnership-pitch-the-6-slide-structure-that-gets-a-lets-move-forward-in-the-first-meeting-f87e3c5d3e6e); similar 4-slide “mutual problem → combined capability → shared economics → decision ask” in [Winning Presentations, 2026](https://winningpresentations.com/partnership-proposal-presentation-one-meeting/)
- Bizzabo 2024 package-design sequence (for the *offer*, not the document cover): understand sponsor goals → segment audience → package customization (platinum to basic) → value-driven unique benefits per tier → innovative activations beyond logo → measure and show ROI. — [Bizzabo 2024 handbook](https://insights.si/wp-content/uploads/bizzabo-2024.-the-strategic-event-sponsorhip-handbook.pdf)

### Inferences
- Recommended order for Abstracthon, collapsing conference 12-section packets into a 2–3 page prospectus:

  1. **Cover / hook** — event name, date, city, one-sentence sponsor promise, projected (labeled) attendance, contact.
  2. **Why this exists / the problem** — talent pipeline, student-builder community, local tech scene gap; one paragraph, not organizer CV.
  3. **Audience** — who is in the room (students, levels, universities, skills, career intent); this is the product.
  4. **Offer** — what a sponsor can *do* with that audience (recruit, product/API adoption, brand-with-builders, CSR/education), not a logo list.
  5. **Packages** — 3 cumulative tiers in a table + exclusivity/custom note.
  6. **Proof / logistics** — MLSA/Microsoft affiliation, past edition or comparable, venue, format, timeline.
  7. **How to become a sponsor** — named person, deadline, what happens after they say yes.

- Cvent’s early “Meet the Team” and TOC are conference-packet habits. CHARGE, EventIQ, DealDuck, Xarify, and partnership-deck advice all push team/org history **after** fit and audience. For a 2–3 page hackathon PDF, a one-line “organized by Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors in Bolivia” on the cover is enough team proof.
- The “problem → audience → offer → packages → next step” narrative the prompt named is the consensus. “Problem” in sponsorship is the *sponsor’s* problem (pipeline, brand among student builders, category exclusivity in a niche), not “we need money to run a hackathon.”

### Gaps
- No source publishes a controlled A/B of section order. The “why” is practitioner consensus (sponsor reads for fit, then evidence, then price), not experimental.
- Splash’s recommended order was not found.

## What must appear in the first 2-3 slides/pages so a sponsor does not bounce?

### Takeaway
The first screen must answer “why should *I* keep reading?” with: (a) what the event is, when, and where; (b) who the audience is, in one number plus one qualifier; (c) why this company, not a generic invite. Logistics dumps, organizer history, and “premier event in [city]” openers are bounce triggers.

### Cited Findings
- DealDuck: most decks open with a venue photo and logo; the reader’s question is “Why should I care?” Best opening: one sentence about the opportunity **for them**, one killer stat, a signal it is not generic (their name, their category). Example formula given. Opening with founding history is Mistake #1. — [DealDuck](https://dealduck.ai/blog/sponsorship-deck-guide/)
- Xarify Mistake 1: the most common opening is “We are excited to invite you to sponsor [Event], the premier [adjective] event in [city].” This “tells the brand nothing they care about.” Lead with their marketing goal, then connect the event. — [Xarify](https://xarify.com/blog/sponsorship-pitch-mistakes-lose-deal-30-seconds.html)
- CHARGE Slide 1: explain why you are reaching **them specifically**. Quote in the article: “If it is not relevant to the sponsor, this is the LAST page they will read.” — [CHARGE](https://chargesponsorship.com/how-to-successfully-build-a-sponsorship-deck-2/)
- Webex element 1 (front of proposal): event name and theme, event goals, why you’re hosting, intended audience type, ideal sponsor type — “respects their time by helping them immediately gauge whether or not this opportunity will be a good fit.” Element 2 (still “toward the beginning”): tailored value proposition. — [Webex 2023 PDF](https://www.webex.com/content/dam/www/us/en/documents/products/pdf/suite/events/webex-events-guide-event-sponsorship-2023.pdf)
- MLH: the basics that “get overlooked” must appear **on all promotional materials**: Event name, Date, Projected attendance, Website URL, Contact email. Story (unique angle) is the other required early block. — [MLH](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- EventIQ first-section mistake: “Opening with your organization's mission when the sponsor came for the audience.” — [EventIQ](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Eventbrite: Event Overview comes first and should be punchy (personality/tone via short film, graphics, or text); do not stray into logistics. Credibility is established “fast through a combination of succinct storytelling, strong visuals, and compelling data.” — [Eventbrite prospectus](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/academy/sample-prospectus-how-to-write-a-winning-event-sponsorship-prospectus/)
- HubSpot: “Use the first page of your proposal to describe the event or project you're organizing and explain how the sponsor would benefit … as well as what you're asking for.” — [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/resources/templates/sponsorship-proposal)
- SponsorFlo Cover: your logo **and theirs**, one positioning line, a real photo of the asset. “A cover with only your logo reads like a mass mailing.” Slide 2 is “Why this brand.” — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pitch-deck-templates)
- Cvent cover: eye-catching theme image; **company name, potential sponsor’s name, and date**. Personalized cover is the first impression. — [Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- Visme: “Too many proposals start with the organizer’s mission or personal story. That’s important but it’s not why a sponsor buys in. Lead with a clear, compelling value proposition: ‘Here’s how sponsoring this initiative will benefit your brand in tangible ways.’” Quantify (e.g. “15,000+ live attendees and 250,000 digital viewers”) rather than “increased exposure.” — [Visme](https://visme.co/blog/sponsorship-proposal-template/)
- ConferenceDatabase: Executive Summary’s job is to “hook the reader immediately with a concise overview of the opportunity and key outcomes.” Biggest mistake (later in the same article): making the proposal about your conference’s needs. — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- AnyGen slide 1: “Signal fit in 5 seconds” — event name, date, city/format, one-sentence sponsor promise. Slide 2: format, topic, audience type, expected attendance, venue. Slide 3: problem/community/moment. — [AnyGen](https://www.anygen.io/showcase/event-sponsorship-deck-template/index.html)
- MakeOnepagers one-pager logic: “A sponsor's first question is who will see their brand. It answers it with the audience numbers up front.” — [MakeOnepagers, Event Sponsorship Proposal One-Pager](https://makeonepagers.com/one-pager-templates/event-sponsorship-proposal)
- Hazeldine partnership transfer: do not spend “three slides on your company, your history, and your awards before the partnership hypothesis ever appears.” — [Hazeldine](https://medium.com/@marybeth.hazeldine/the-partnership-pitch-the-6-slide-structure-that-gets-a-lets-move-forward-in-the-first-meeting-f87e3c5d3e6e)

### Inferences
- First 2–3 pages for Abstracthon should contain, visibily, not buried:

  1. Abstracthon 2026 · date · Bolivia / city · format (hackathon, duration) · named contact.
  2. Audience in one line that a brand manager can forward: e.g. “N university students / early-career builders, [skills], from [cities/universities]” — labeled as target vs last-edition fact.
  3. Sponsor-side promise in one sentence (talent, product in students’ hands, category exclusivity, education/CSR with Microsoft Learn affiliation).
  4. Why *them* if the PDF is customized; if the master PDF is generic, put the “why you” in the carta and keep the PDF’s first page about audience + uniqueness.

- Institutional affiliation (MLSA / Microsoft) belongs on page 1 as **credibility**, not as three slides of ambassador program history.
- Personalized cover (their logo + name) is the conference/enterprise standard (Cvent, SponsorFlo, DealDuck). For 40 local prospects, a master PDF plus a tailored carta is the realistic campus version; fully unique covers are for the 5–10 priority accounts.

### Gaps
- No source measured bounce by first-slide content for hackathons specifically.
- DealDuck “11 seconds” is unsourced; treat as rhetorical, not a KPI.

## What social proof, audience data, and logistics do sponsors expect to see?

### Takeaway
Sponsors buy a **described audience** plus evidence you can deliver and measure. They expect: who is in the room (role, skill, geography, intent), how you know, how many actually showed up vs registered, what similar brands got last time, and the operational facts (date, venue, format, promotions, reporting). Logo walls, merged “reach” numbers, and undifferentiated “tech professionals” claims fail this test. First-year events substitute affiliation, pipeline (pre-registrations), and comparable events — MLH said as much.

### Cited Findings
- **Audience as the product.** CHARGE: demographics (gender, age, residence, income) + psychographics + **buying behavior**; “A sponsor selling home improvement products cares far less about your audience’s age than whether they’re homeowners considering a renovation.” Marketing slide needs specific figures (“42,000 Instagram followers at 4.8% engagement”), not “strong social presence.” — [CHARGE](https://chargesponsorship.com/how-to-successfully-build-a-sponsorship-deck-2/)
- ConferenceDatabase: vague “business leaders / tech professionals” causes sponsors to tune out. They want job titles, industries, purchasing power, decision-maker vs influencer. Visual snapshot example: 45% C-Suite/VP, top industries, company size, geo split. — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- Eventbrite: “forensic level of detail”; demographics from surveys, social insights, Eventbrite analytics; lifestyles/spend where they overlap the sponsor; visual personas; **size and growth rate**. — [Eventbrite prospectus](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/academy/sample-prospectus-how-to-write-a-winning-event-sponsorship-prospectus/)
- Eventbrite pitch deck (2022): be specific on expected headcount; in-person vs virtual; dates, location, speakers, broadcast tech; attendee quotes; stats from previous events (attendees, speakers, origin). — [Eventbrite pitch deck](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/ds00-perfecting-your-sponsorship-pitch-deck/)
- EventIQ measurement discipline (B2B, 2026): registrations and attendance as **two counts**; every figure dated and sourced; do not mix last year’s measured counts with this year’s target in one table; do not sum attendees + newsletter + social into one “audience”; say what you do not know (empty optional fields). Forecasts under their own heading. — [EventIQ](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Webex past-event metrics sponsors “would probably like to see”: leads generated by sponsors, follow-up engagements, clicks on sponsored elements, sponsored session participation, past sponsor revenue/ROI. Audience: size, demographics, buying power, ticket sales. — [Webex 2023 PDF](https://www.webex.com/content/dam/www/us/en/documents/products/pdf/suite/events/webex-events-guide-event-sponsorship-2023.pdf)
- Who's In ROI metrics that “close sponsors”: lead capture of opted-in scans; session attendance from room counts; brand impressions listed in a post-event report; NPS/recall survey questions; promo-code attribution; live lead lists. “A general audience of 1,000 is worth less … than 200 senior decision-makers in their vertical.” — [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/resources/sponsor-prospectus-template)
- Bizzabo 2024: sponsors demand data on attendee engagement, lead generation, brand exposure; they want social proof that **similar brands** succeeded; case studies/testimonials. Women In Product / Event Industry News quotes in the handbook: sponsors now ask agenda, floor time, who is there, industries, seniority, decision-makers, and in-person analytics. — [Bizzabo 2024 handbook](https://insights.si/wp-content/uploads/bizzabo-2024.-the-strategic-event-sponsorhip-handbook.pdf)
- Xarify Mistake 3: “We expect 5,000 attendees” is meaningless without who/earn/buy/live/care. Mistake 5: no testimonials, photos, retention, or previous sponsor names = blind leap; even one quote + “70% of our sponsors renew” changes risk. — [Xarify](https://xarify.com/blog/sponsorship-pitch-mistakes-lose-deal-30-seconds.html)
- DealDuck: translate demographics into the brand’s language (“62% purchased athletic apparel in the last 90 days”); every data point needs the number, the context, and the so-what; 3–4 partner **stories with results**, not 47 logos. — [DealDuck](https://dealduck.ai/blog/sponsorship-deck-guide/)
- Visme: attendance growth, social reach/engagement, CTR on prior sponsor content, post-event satisfaction or sponsor recall; logos, testimonials, activation screenshots, media/influencer mentions. Mini one-pager of past sponsor results. — [Visme](https://visme.co/blog/sponsorship-proposal-template/)
- Cvent: past-event photos, previous-year sponsor logos (so they can see similar companies), attendee infographic; optional quotes from speakers/attendees. — [Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- IU 2023 community prospectus: expected turnout, intended impact, marketing/promotion details, costs so the sponsor knows the extent of the ask; logo reach (web and print); engagement options (speaker, booth, giveaways); VIP/employee benefits. Visuals of past events. — [IUScholarWorks 2023](https://hdl.handle.net/2022/29523)
- MLH pre-ask “you should definitely have”: a vision, a website, pre-registrations; venue and other sponsors marked as progress-even-if-not-locked. “Sponsors are like investors — you should approach them when you’re doing well.” Itemized budget; know where the money goes. Prospectus should explain **what a hackathon is** and show how sponsors have been involved at other hackathons. — [MLH Hackcon workshop](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mlh-hackcon-iv-hackathon-sponsorship-101-workshop/63544317); [MLH prospectus](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Hacktech 2025 logistics/social-proof style: 36-hour format; mission (next generation of engineers); how funds are used (logistics, travel stipends, promotional materials); branding + **recruiting** as co-equal columns (resumes, recruiters onsite, interviews, exclusive student hour, judging). — [Hacktech 2025](https://www.hack.caltech.edu/2025/2025sponsor.pdf)
- AAAI 2025: audience line (“AI researchers, developers, students, and industry professionals”); four outcomes (brand with AAAI, recruitment, APIs in real projects, networking); resume/GitHub access at top tier; Discord pre-event engagement; judging; workshops. — [AAAI 2025](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Hackathon-Sponsorship-2025.pdf)
- Mosaic Lowell 2024: if first year, “only include your goals”; if recurring, show last year by the numbers **and** this year’s estimated goals as separate blocks (example: 600+ attendance last year → 700+ goal). — [Mosaic Lowell 2024 template](https://mosaiclowell.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/TEMPLATE_Event-Sponsorship-Packet.pdf)
- EventIQ / privacy: do not promise a contact list your registration terms do not permit; distinguish aggregate vs identifiable; named reporting date before the renewal conversation. — [EventIQ](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Who's In on timing: 8–10 months for corporates, 6 months for smaller companies, never <4 months — **conference assumption**. — [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/resources/sponsor-prospectus-template)
- ConferenceDatabase timing: 6–12 months for large corporates; 3–6 months for smaller/nimble; align with fiscal year. Across 745 conferences with USD prices (snapshot 29 Sep 2026), **median entry-level package $2,500** (conference market, not hackathons). — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)

### Inferences
- For a **hackathon**, the audience data that transfers is not C-suite purchasing power. It is: university mix, year of study, skill stacks, career intent (intern/full-time/startup), project domains, geographic catchment, gender/inclusion stats if collected ethically, and **what students will do with a sponsor’s API/cloud/tool**. Recruiting access (resumes with consent, booth, judging, talk) is the native “lead capture.”
- First-year / small-N Abstracthon substitutes: MLSA + Microsoft Learn affiliation; named venue if locked; pre-registration count (MLH); comparable Bolivian/LatAm student events; photos from prior community events if not prior Abstracthon; 3–4 relevant past partners as one-line stories rather than a logo graveyard.
- Separate **target** from **measured**. EventIQ’s rule is the one that will keep a first-year deck honest.
- Conference “buying power / ticket sales” metrics do not map; do not invent them. If you cannot measure leads, promise what you *can* count: booth visits, workshop attendance, opt-in talent list, GitHub/demo links, social mentions, swag uptake.
- B2B finance-grade sections (shortfall terms, impression methodology, CRM join keys) are EventIQ/association conference machinery. A campus prospectus can cover the same *idea* in two lines: what you will report, by when, and that personal data is opt-in.

## What are common mistakes (too much about the organizers, no CTA, vague benefits, no exclusivity, too many slides)?

### Takeaway
The pitch dies when it is an organizer brochure: mission slides, logo-on-banner as the hero benefit, vague “exposure,” no price context, no exclusivity, no next step, and a document so long it is never forwarded. The same sources also warn against the opposite campus failure: a wall of text, a generic mass PDF, and treating an inventory list as a proposal.

### Cited Findings
- **About the organizers / about the event’s needs.** ConferenceDatabase “single biggest mistake”: making the proposal about your conference. Frame every page as “what’s in it for us?” from the sponsor’s seat. — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- DealDuck Mistake #1: three slides of history. Mistake #2: logo graveyard of 47 partners signals you are not selective. Mistake #3: generic demographics with no translation. Mistake #4: listing inventory (signage, PA, posts) instead of a 3-hour experience story. Mistake #5: price with no context (invites discounting). Mistake #6: 80-slide “everything deck”; cap 12–18; the deck is a trailer to get a meeting. Mistake #7: no CTA/contact/meeting link. — [DealDuck](https://dealduck.ai/blog/sponsorship-deck-guide/)
- Xarify: opening with the event not their problem; sending rates before value (HBR B2B sequence claim — not independently retrieved); generic headcount; **logo placement as lead benefit** (“Logo visibility is a commodity”); no social proof; wall-of-text Word docs; one-and-done follow-up. Practical cap: 10 slides, one idea each. — [Xarify](https://xarify.com/blog/sponsorship-pitch-mistakes-lose-deal-30-seconds.html)
- EventIQ mistake column (one per section): mission-first opening; mixing target with measured audience; merging registrations/attendance/newsletter; tiers that differ **only in logo size** (invites a discount); price with no build-up; promising uncounted units; blank “will not count”; guarantee with no remedy; promising a list privacy policy forbids; no report date; hiding modest last-year numbers; deadlines with no post-signature steps. — [EventIQ](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- CHARGE: cookie-cutter decks “land in the trash”; if you don’t know needs, research should still let you guess; logo on website “may not be adequate”; do not leave the sponsor to infer ROI. — [CHARGE](https://chargesponsorship.com/how-to-successfully-build-a-sponsorship-deck-2/)
- Eventbrite pitch deck: **offer exclusivity** — only one silver, or only one breakfast sponsor. Create a **different deck per tier** rather than dumping every level on every prospect (this is a large-event tactic). Respect time; thank them; include when *you* will follow up. — [Eventbrite pitch deck](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/ds00-perfecting-your-sponsorship-pitch-deck/)
- Webex: limited inventory per tier creates exclusivity and FOMO (example table: Bronze 10 / Silver 5 / Gold 2). Pitfall of rigid packages: sponsors pay for unused features; mitigate with a custom tier. Don’t hide price. — [Webex 2023 PDF](https://www.webex.com/content/dam/www/us/en/documents/products/pdf/suite/events/webex-events-guide-event-sponsorship-2023.pdf)
- EventsCase: limited slots / exclusivities belong **inside** each package description; generic emails and generic attachments are called out. — [EventsCase](https://eventscase.es/blog/como-pedir-patrocinio-para-un-evento-guia)
- Sponsorship Collective mistakes: too much information; sending proposals too early (before discovery); treating packages as proposals; ignoring audience data; generic messaging. — [Sponsorship Collective](https://sponsorshipcollective.com/blog/sponsorship-deck-vs-one-pager-vs-proposal/)
- SponsorFlo mistakes: generic deck with no sponsor branding on cover; too many slides (8–12 enough); hiding prices; audience claims without source; ending on “Thank you” with no action. — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pitch-deck-templates)
- Visme: rigid bronze-silver-gold without à la carte or custom; unquantified exposure; no homework on the brand. Alternative: label tiers by **outcome** (“Community Impact Partner”) not only metal. 3–4 anchor tiers + à la carte + presenting-sponsor exclusivity. — [Visme](https://visme.co/blog/sponsorship-proposal-template/)
- Bizzabo 2024: one-size-fits-all packages are giving way to customized/à la carte; logo placement is no longer enough vs activations; CSR/DEI/sustainability alignment is now a sponsor filter; asking a company in a layoff/rebrand at the wrong time is tone-deaf. — [Bizzabo 2024 handbook](https://insights.si/wp-content/uploads/bizzabo-2024.-the-strategic-event-sponsorhip-handbook.pdf)
- ConferenceDatabase on Gold/Silver/Bronze: “played out” if it tells nothing about value; entry = awareness, mid = lead gen, top = exclusive access/thought leadership. Average event sponsorship deal “hovers around $12,000” in their industry-data aside (conference market). — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- MLH: too-long decks; non-legible / too much text; missing basics; most expensive tier > 25% of budget; non-cumulative tiers. First email with attachments gets auto-trashed. — [MLH](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Partnership-deck transfer mistakes: one-sided value; 30-slide first conversation; vague “let’s explore”; no decision ask. — [slide-deck.io, Strategic Partnership Proposal Deck](https://slide-deck.io/blog/strategic-partnership-proposal-deck); [Hazeldine](https://medium.com/@marybeth.hazeldine/the-partnership-pitch-the-6-slide-structure-that-gets-a-lets-move-forward-in-the-first-meeting-f87e3c5d3e6e)

### Inferences
- For Abstracthon, the high-probability kills are: (1) three pages about MLSA/the team; (2) benefits that are only logo size; (3) “patrocina la innovación” with no audience numbers; (4) four-plus metal tiers for a 100–300 person event; (5) no exclusive category (e.g. only one cloud, one bank, one telco at the top); (6) PDF that ends with “gracias” and a generic inbox; (7) attaching a 20-slide PPTX to a cold email.
- Exclusivity at campus scale is **category exclusivity + limited top-tier inventory**, not “only 2 Gold packages of 10 left” theater copied from Webex’s 5,000-person table.
- Tiers that only change logo size are the EventIQ discount magnet. Each step up should unlock a different *job*: shoutout → booth/mentors/API challenge → title/keynote/resume access (Hacktech/AAAI/MLH pattern).
- “Too many slides” for this event means anything that cannot survive as a 2–3 page PDF. Conference 12–18 slide advice is the *ceiling for a live meeting deck*, not a target.

### Gaps
- No source quantified conversion drop-off per extra slide for student hackathons.
- Sports-only “logo graveyard / NASCAR hood” metaphor is DealDuck; the principle (selectivity + case studies) transfers, the stadium examples do not.

## How do good decks handle contact, deadline, and "how to become a sponsor"?

### Takeaway
The close is a **named human + a date + one action + what happens after they say yes**. Deadlines are framed as inventory/production cutoffs (logos, exclusivity, early-bird), not fake FOMO. Hackathon prospectuses put the email on every page and invite custom/in-kind at the foot of the tier table. The carta carries the first ask; the PDF makes it easy to forward internally and still know whom to reply to.

### Cited Findings
- CHARGE Slide 8: not “let us know if you have questions.” Propose a date/time for a call, an invitation, or a clear action. “The goal of the deck is to set a meeting.” — [CHARGE](https://chargesponsorship.com/how-to-successfully-build-a-sponsorship-deck-2/)
- DealDuck last slide: one person’s name, email, Calendly, one sentence (“Let’s find 15 minutes…”). — [DealDuck](https://dealduck.ai/blog/sponsorship-deck-guide/)
- Eventbrite pitch deck tip 15: contact information, how they follow up, **and when you will reach back out**. Thank them. — [Eventbrite pitch deck](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/ds00-perfecting-your-sponsorship-pitch-deck/)
- ConferenceDatabase CTAs to copy: “To secure the Platinum Partnership for the 2025 Summit, please sign and return this agreement by October 15th.” / “Let’s schedule a 15-minute call next week to finalize the details.” Follow-up: ~1 week after send, then another 1–2 weeks; helpful not demanding. File = PDF. — [ConferenceDatabase](https://conferencedatabase.com/blog/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- Cvent Page 9 Deadline: create FOMO; include early-bird discounts; a deadline makes follow-up easier. Pages 10+: legal T&Cs (grant of rights, obligations, liability) — large-event legal. Page 12+: space to **sign**, thank-you, social links. Optional short feedback questions. — [Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-sponsorship-proposal-template)
- EventIQ last section: sign-by dates, the person who answers questions, **and what happens in the week after signature**. Mistake: “Deadlines with none of the steps that follow them.” Also set the post-event report date before renewal talks. — [EventIQ](https://eventiq.io/templates/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Who's In: fifth section is “a simple one-page booking form.” — [Who's In](https://whos-in.app/resources/sponsor-prospectus-template)
- AnyGen slides 10–12: fulfillment timeline (decision date, logo deadline, payment deadline, attendee-list rules, reporting date) → reporting plan → named contact, 20-minute sponsor-fit call, decision deadline. — [AnyGen](https://www.anygen.io/showcase/event-sponsorship-deck-template/index.html)
- SponsorFlo next step: “One clear ask: book a call, reply, or sign.” Avoid ending on Thank You. — [SponsorFlo](https://www.sponsorflo.ai/resources/sponsorship-pitch-deck-templates)
- MLH: contact email on **all** materials; custom packages “upon further negotiation with your team's email address” at the **bottom of the tier overview page**. After exploratory call, send proposal with **a deadline** and ask for a follow-up call; “Less is more.” Process: research → get in touch → exploratory call → proposal → follow up. — [MLH prospectus](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus); [MLH Hackcon workshop](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mlh-hackcon-iv-hackathon-sponsorship-101-workshop/63544317)
- Hacktech: “Feel free to contact us for any questions or negotiations: we are flexible!” Close of letter + “Reach out to hacktech@caltech.edu!” on the matrix. — [Hacktech 2025](https://www.hack.caltech.edu/2025/2025sponsor.pdf)
- AAAI: named Sponsorship Chair with university email **and** aaai25@aaai.org; “We are open to tailoring… non-monetary contributions, branded prize categories.” — [AAAI 2025](https://aaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Hackathon-Sponsorship-2025.pdf)
- EventsCase carta contents: quién eres, qué buscas, por qué les contactas a ellos, qué propones, **cuál es el siguiente paso**. Paso 6: reminders, availability for a short call; after the event, full report + renewal ask. — [EventsCase](https://eventscase.es/blog/como-pedir-patrocinio-para-un-evento-guia)
- Bizzabo letter template: named sender, org, event name/date/venue; why this company; bullet benefits; “I have attached our comprehensive sponsorship package”; “I’d love to discuss… further”; signature with name, title, organization. Subject line pattern: “Partnership Opportunity: Sponsorship for [Event name].” — [Bizzabo 2024 handbook](https://insights.si/wp-content/uploads/bizzabo-2024.-the-strategic-event-sponsorhip-handbook.pdf)
- Visme follow-up sequence: initial send → 3 days → 7–10 days → close-the-loop ask to connect; optional short video email walking through the proposal. — [Visme](https://visme.co/blog/sponsorship-proposal-template/)
- Xarify follow-up: initial → 5-day check-in with a specific question → 10-day value-add → final ask at three weeks, then stop. Non-response ≠ no; multiple internal approvers. — [Xarify](https://xarify.com/blog/sponsorship-pitch-mistakes-lose-deal-30-seconds.html)
- IU 2023: condensed one-pager first, longer tailored version on follow-up. — [IUScholarWorks](https://hdl.handle.net/2022/29523)
- Pricing-in-the-close conflict (how they buy): Webex “Don’t try to be sly” — outline packages and pricing, note customization. ConferenceDatabase: omitting price is “a classic mistake.” SponsorFlo: hiding prices forces a meeting just to learn the cost. CHARGE/Xarify: omit investment slide on first outreach; meeting first. Hackathon packets (MLH, Hacktech, AAAI) print prices. — sources as cited above
- HubSpot conclusion: highlight benefits **and describe the next steps**. — [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/resources/templates/sponsorship-proposal)

### Inferences
- Abstracthon “how to become a sponsor” block, on the last page and repeated as a footer:

  - Named person + role (not a generic Gmail if avoidable) + WhatsApp/phone if that is how Bolivian SMEs actually reply.
  - One primary action: “Responde este PDF eligiendo un paquete” **or** “agenda 20 minutos” — pick one primary, not three equals.
  - Hard dates: (1) decision/exclusivity hold; (2) logo/artwork; (3) payment or in-kind delivery; (4) optional early-bird.
  - After yes: invoice/agreement, logo specs, on-site point of contact, what they get the week after (standup with organizers).
  - Custom/in-kind sentence under the tier table (MLH/Hacktech/AAAI).
  - Do not put Cvent-style T&Cs and a signature block in a 3-page campus PDF; send a one-page agreement after they choose a tier.

- Carta vs PDF: the carta is where “why you” and the meeting ask live (EventsCase, Bizzabo, MLH’s no-attachment first email). The PDF must still be understandable if forwarded without the email (DealDuck).
- For Microsoft-ecosystem or multinational prospects, use the long corporate clock (months). For local sponsors, a shorter deadline tied to production (shirts, badges, website lock) is more honest than a fake “only 2 Gold left.”

### Gaps
- No source specified WhatsApp/in-person closing norms for Bolivia; EventsCase is Spain-based and assumes email + call.
- Splash’s booking-flow recommendations were not found.
- Whether to include a signable last page: Cvent yes (conference contract packet); MLH/Hacktech no (email to negotiate). For Abstracthon, evidence favors email/form over in-PDF signature.
