# Valor que un sponsor compra en un hackathon

Context for the report-writer (not a finding): Abstracthon 2026 is a Bolivia student/community hackathon organized by the Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors University Community; in-person in Santa Cruz and Oruro (Cochabamba mentioned internally); tracks Finanzas, Educación, Comunicación, Ayuda tecnológica; Friday virtual / Saturday in-person / Sunday pitch. **Do not invent or estimate Abstracthon attendance.** No public, sourced headcount for Abstracthon 2026 was found in this research pass.

## Why do companies sponsor hackathons specifically (vs a conference or a social-media shoutout)?

### Takeaway
Sponsors buy a weekend of **hands-on contact with builders** — recruiting by watching people ship, product adoption by putting an API into real projects, and brand/perception change with a developer audience that is hard to reach through ads or a conference booth. Logo placement and a social shoutout are the cheapest, weakest form of that trade; the conversion happens when the company *uses* the room.

### Cited Findings
- MLH’s organizer guide states sponsors support hackathons for **three primary reasons**: (1) real-time product feedback, especially on APIs and development tools (buyer: DevRel / Developer Advocate); (2) recruiting interns and full-time employees, especially in the fall (buyer: Hiring / Talent / HR); (3) raising awareness or **changing perception** of a brand, product, or service (buyer: Marketing / PR / employee resource groups). Additional motives: community-value alignment, product engagement, and **region-targeted goals** for companies with a local campus/career-fair presence. — [MLH: Understanding Your Sponsors](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/understanding-your-sponsors)
- The same MLH page frames the pitch as a **sales pitch, not a donation ask**, and lists sponsors’ top practical concerns in order: **attendee value / unique bridge to a hard-to-reach audience**, who else is sponsoring, package value, and gut-feel competence of the organizers. — [MLH: Understanding Your Sponsors](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/understanding-your-sponsors)
- AngelHack’s sponsorship guide (last updated Jan 2019; still the most explicit “why hackathon vs booth” write-up found) lists the same three goals — recruiting, brand exposure, product feedback — and contrasts immersive hackathon experiences with “walking past a billboard with your logo on it or just another static stall at a conference.” Example KPIs they give sponsors: find 5 candidates interested in a role; 100 new emails; 20 product-feedback forms. They also list R&D-adjacent motives: discover creative uses of the tech, prototype channels, reduce time-to-market, identify new partners. — [AngelHack: Be an All Star Sponsor](https://angelhack.com/blog/be-an-all-star-sponsor-at-your-next-hackathon/)
- Peer-reviewed evidence (data: 1,302 developers at 167 hackathons, Jan 2014–May 2017, 29 platform sponsors): after attending a hackathon **sponsored by a given platform**, a developer’s annual hazard of adopting that platform’s API rose by **20.4 percentage points** vs developers who attended no hackathon or one without that sponsor. Social learning also mattered: every 10-point increase in the share of attendees already using a platform was associated with a 1.2-point increase in others’ adoption hazard the following year. The mechanism is in-person social coordination (workshops, competitions, informal hangouts), not logo impressions. Paper: Pan Fang, Wu, Clough, *Strategic Management Journal* (2021). — [Rice Business Wisdom summary](https://business.rice.edu/wisdom/peer-reviewed-research/tech-companies-should-sponsor-hackathons); [SMJ DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.3230)
- University packets sell the same three outcomes in sponsor language. Hack@Davidson’s 2026/27 sponsor page leads with “More than a logo” and three buyer jobs: recruiting pipeline, marketing reach, **product adoption via a sponsored prize track plus a post-event report on how tools were used**. — [Hack@Davidson Sponsor](https://hackatdavidson.com/sponsor)
- HackRU (Rutgers, 26th edition) titles its benefits “Influence Tomorrow’s Engineers” and lists booths, workshops, and **custom challenge + API distribution** as the conversion path (“convert participants into long-term users and advocates”). — [HackRU Sponsorship](https://sponsor.hackru.org/)
- A 2026 student-organizer playbook states the trade explicitly: “Sponsorship isn’t charity; it’s a trade.” Four sponsor wants: reach, product adoption, brand with the next generation of engineers, hiring. Reframe: not “help us run our fest” but “you’ll get N CS students using your API over a weekend, a workshop slot, and a branded prize track.” — [NamoID: How to Get Sponsors](https://namoid.in/blog/how-to-get-sponsors-for-your-hackathon) (dated 2026-07-09)
- Gupta’s  organizer guide: “A sponsorship budget is a **marketing line item, not a donation**.” Pitch a challenge track (API in builders’ hands for years), a judging seat (reason for engineers to show up), and a named recap mention (backlink / case study they didn’t write). Distinguishes **enterprise/CSR sponsors** (community-relations / social-good budget; pitch visible community impact) from **dev-tool vendors** (API adoption). — [Gupta: Hackathon Sponsorship Guide](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- Unstop’s 2026 hiring comparison: hackathons are strong when a company needs to **evaluate a demonstrable skill across a pool in a short period** and also wants employer branding; internships win for sustained observation. Registrations are a vanity metric if both a 5,000-reg and a 500-reg event produce the same number of hires. — [Unstop: Hackathon vs Internship Hiring](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-vs-internship-hiring)
- Unstop’s metrics guide: “A successful corporate hackathon isn’t measured by registrations alone.” Conversion, cost-per-hire (including mentor/judge time), offer acceptance, and retention beat attendance. They report that some hackathon hiring programs have claimed **interview-to-hire conversion above 60%**; industry offer-acceptance averages around 56% (these are platform-blog figures, not an independent audit). — [Unstop: 10 Metrics](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-success-metrics)
- Events-based recruiting write-up (WinTech): hackathons are the format for **technical / data / product roles** (KPIs: qualified leads, challenge submissions, post-event hires); conferences are for senior/niche/global talent (lead capture, booth traffic) at higher cost and more diluted attention. Cited LinkedIn Global Recruiting Trends 2023 as the KPI source for event-to-hire conversion of 3–8% as a “best practice” range — that figure is **second-hand via this blog**, not verified against the LinkedIn report in this pass. — [WinTech: Events Based Recruiting](https://wintechub.com/events-based-recruiting-hackathons-meetups-and-conferences/)
- GuadalaHacks (student hackathon at Tecnológico de Monterrey Guadalajara, expected 251–500) states the campus-fair contrast in organizer voice: “The next leaders … are **not at job fairs or in classrooms**. They are solving problems … at hackathons.” — [GuadalaHacks SponsorMyEvent](https://www.sponsormyevent.com/guadalahacks-guadalajara)
- Hack Western 13th-edition packet (Nov 2026; 320 participants, 82 projects at HW12) claims **47% of participants who connected with sponsors were later hired directly by those companies**, and **85% of attendees interacted with 3+ sponsor booths**. These are **self-reported organizer claims**, not a third-party ROI study. — [Hack Western Sponsorship Package](https://www.hackwestern.com/shared/sponsors/sponsors.pdf)
- CSR/education is a **fourth budget**, not a substitute for the first three. Northland Hackathon’s CSR pitch (first-party, selling sponsorship) maps grassroots coding events to talent-pipeline, ESG/SDG reporting (students reached, demographics, geography), brand affinity (“students remember who showed up when they were learning to code”), and employee-volunteer mentorship. — [Northland: CSR Tech Education](https://northlandhackathon.com/corporate-social-responsibility-tech-education)
- Hack4Impact McGill’s 2023–24 packet is the student-nonprofit version of the same mix: recruit motivated students **and** “demonstrate philanthropic responsibility.” — [Hack4Impact McGill Sponsorship PDF](https://mcgill.hack4impact.org/static/sponsorship.pdf)
- Hack@CEWIT 2024 (8th edition, “more than 300 students”) asks sponsors to “leverage student brainpower to build a quick prototype,” explore unique interpretations of existing technology, raise brand recognition, and recruit. — [Hack@CEWIT 2024 Sponsorship PDF](https://www.stonybrook.edu/commcms/cewit/hackatcewit2024/_pdf/Hack%2024_Sponsorship.pdf)

### Inferences
- The thing a hackathon uniquely sells vs a **conference** is **observation of work under time pressure** plus **forced product use** (challenge/API). A conference sells talks, badges, and booth traffic among a mixed professional audience. A **social shoutout** sells impressions with no usage, no CVs, no feedback, and no hiring signal.
- Buyer mapping for Abstracthon 2026: local banks/fintechs and telcos → hiring + track ownership (Finanzas / Comunicación); edtech and universities → Educación + CSR/education; software/cloud/API vendors → DevRel adoption via workshop + challenge; large local employers with RSE budgets → CSR + student pipeline, pitched in ESG language, not “vibes.”
- “Mission, community, energy” is useful only as **color after** attendee-fit is proven. MLH’s #1 sponsor concern is whether attendees are a good recruiting/marketing fit and whether the event is a **unique bridge**. Vibes do not answer that.
- Do not quote Hack Western’s 47% hire rate or Unstop’s 60% interview-to-hire as Abstracthon promises. Those are mature/corporate-hiring events. The transferable idea is: **sponsors measure hires, API usage, and conversations**, not “community.”

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 independent (non-organizer, non-platform) ROI study of **small LATAM student hackathons** was found. The 20.4% platform-adoption result uses 2014–2017 North American hackathon data.
- Direct head-to-head budget studies of “hackathon sponsorship vs conference booth vs paid social” for the same company were not found; the conference contrast is argued in organizer/DevRel language, not in audited media-mix models.
- LinkedIn Global Recruiting Trends 2023 was not retrieved first-hand; treat the 3–8% event-to-hire range as unverified.

## What proof points work when you do not have huge attendance numbers yet (community, universities, MLSA, photos, past events, organizing team)?

### Takeaway
When headcount is small or first-edition, sponsors buy **proof of access and delivery competence**, not a fake crowd. Named universities, a real organizing team, labeled photos from *this community’s* past events, MLSA/university affiliation, confirmed venues, and a promised post-event report outperform adjectives (“vibrant,” “the biggest,” “mission-driven”).

### Cited Findings
- MLH prospectus spec: **2–3 pages**, legible, minimum text. Required on all materials: event name, date, **projected attendance**, website, contact email. Also: “the story behind your event” = **brief intro to the team**, what you are trying to accomplish, and **what makes the event unique**. Three tiers (reasonable / moderate / expensive); most expensive **not more than 25% of budget**; custom packages available. “Sponsors generally don’t have time to read through a big sponsorship deck.” — [MLH: Sponsorship Prospectus](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- MLH: for **marketing/perception** buyers, “Photos from previous events will help more with these pitches.” For region-targeted local companies, “send **specific stats** about your event to help their process.” Gut-feel concern: is the event well-organized; have you been professional in communications? — [MLH: Understanding Your Sponsors](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/understanding-your-sponsors)
- MLH 5-step process: on the exploratory call, “**Sell your team and your story.** What makes your hackathon special?” Help the sponsor understand what a hackathon is (analogy: science fair). Ask **their** success metrics from previous sponsorships. Diversify outreach: previous local event sponsors, LinkedIn, **university career services / companies that already sponsor campus job fairs**, startups + midsize + large. — [MLH: 5-Step Sponsorship Process](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/the-5-step-mlh-sponsorship-process)
- MLH email cheat sheet: first things sponsors look for are **size, location, dates**. Also: a one-line “what a hackathon is,” an **engaging angle tied to that company**, and what makes the event unique (example they give: “largest female-centric event in the east”). First email should **never** contain links/attachments (IT phishing filters). — [MLH: Email Cheat Sheet](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/cheat-sheet-emailing-your-sponsors)
- First-edition commercial advice (Loopyah, 2026): you can sell a first event **without last year’s breakdown**. Answer the question behind the ask: will the **right people** come, can the sponsor **do something useful**, can you **deliver**. Do not disguise the gap. Dated evidence beats a forecast: current registrations/tickets, interest list, audience survey, university/department backing, venue booking. Offer a **smaller activity with reporting** rather than a headline package built on an ambitious attendance forecast. — [Loopyah: First event sponsorship](https://loopyah.com/blog/selling/first-event-sponsorship)
- Loopyah on team proof: show relevant team experience **accurately** (role + work actually done). A venue contact confirming a previous installation speaks to delivery; it does **not** prove ticket demand for the new event. Do not caption another event’s photo as this one. Do not put a “supported by” logo before the sponsor has agreed. — [Loopyah: First event sponsorship](https://loopyah.com/blog/selling/first-event-sponsorship)
- NamoID 2026 FAQ: “What if we have no past editions? Lead with your **institution’s brand**, a realistic projected attendance, your **organising team’s credibility**, and **faculty/placement-cell backing**. Offer a strong first-year discount in exchange for a **testimonial and case study**.” “Numbers beat adjectives. ‘600 registrations, 220 finalists, 40 colleges last year’ does more than a paragraph of ‘India’s most vibrant hackathon.’” — [NamoID](https://namoid.in/blog/how-to-get-sponsors-for-your-hackathon)
- UK student-hackathon organizer guide: first cash often comes from the **CS department**, local council culture funds, **large-company student developer programs (explicitly: Microsoft)**, university spinoffs, and the **department advisory board** (“full of companies who want to help students”). — [UK Hackathons: Sponsorship](https://hack.athon.uk/organise/before/sponsorship/)
- Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors public positioning: ambassadors are **community builders** who host events online and locally and **change makers** who organize events; program benefits include Microsoft 365, VS Enterprise, **$150 USD/month Azure credits**, badges, swag. Open to students across degree fields. — [Microsoft Learn: Become a Student Ambassador](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/student-hub/become-a-student-ambassador)
- Official MLSA community-engagement playbook: global student community; ambassadors lead local communities, host campus/online talks, co-host with local ambassadors, follow up with Discord/LinkedIn. Walking deck and one-pager last updated **January 2024**. — [MLSA Community Engagement Playbook](https://microsoft.github.io/MicrosoftLearnStudentAmbassadors/)
- MLSA CIT chapter site (India, public) is a working example of **chapter-level proof without a mega-hackathon**: named past workshops with **real counts** (e.g. “The Zeitgeist… 350+ participants”; UI/UX “100+ attendees”; dated Git/GitHub and LinkedIn workshops in 2024–2025). — [MLSA CIT](https://mlsacit.vercel.app/)
- MLSA LATAM public GitHub org exists (created 2022); profile page lists named ambassadors including **Anahi Sanabria, Beta MLSA, Cochabamba, Bolivia**. — [MLSA LATAM GitHub](https://github.com/MLSA-LATAM); [MLSA LATAM profile](https://mlsa-latam.github.io/.github/profile/)
- Closest public **Bolivia first-edition student-tech event** analog: AWS **Student Community Day Cochabamba 2026** (“Sponsor the **first** Student Community Day in Bolivia”) does **not** invent SCD history. It stacks the new event on **AWS Community Day Bolivia** growth: 2024 La Paz 647 registered / 395 attendees / 27 speakers; 2025 Cochabamba 1,100+ registered / 592 attendees / 31 speakers; 2026 Santa Cruz 2,122 registered / 956 attendees / 59 speakers. Expected SCD audience is stated as **900+** with **12+ universities**. Packages USD 300–1,000. Gold+ get a **report within 15 days** (attendance, digital reach, activation metrics). Host/Platinum include **opt-in CVs**, talent fair, workshop, **branded challenge**. — [AWS SCD Bolivia sponsor deck](https://bolivia.studentcommunity.day/en/sponsor-deck)
- ScarletHacks 2026 (Illinois Tech; social-equity mission) still sells **audience geography** (Illinois Tech + Chicago-area colleges + community colleges), date, duration, team size, and **named tracks** before mission copy. Tiers start at $500–$749. — [ScarletHacks 2026 Sponsorship Package](https://www.scarlethacks.com/SponsorshipPackage_ScarletHacks2026-4.pdf)
- Hack Western, a 13-year event, shows what “proof” looks like **once you have it**: 320 participants; 82 projects; school/program/year/gender breakdowns; Western entrepreneurship ranking; $2.3B raised by Western-founded companies in 2022. First-edition events cannot copy this page. — [Hack Western packet](https://www.hackwestern.com/shared/sponsors/sponsors.pdf)
- Organizer Kassidy Webb (CrimsonHacks): companies sponsor more when you show **goals and past successes**; personal relationships beat cold “please sponsor”; do **not** attach the packet on first contact if the company has never sponsored a hackathon — they may not know what one is. — [Medium: Tips for Getting Hackathon Sponsors](https://medium.com/@kassidywebb/tips-for-getting-hackathon-sponsors-4bfba16fd4ec)

### Inferences
- For Abstracthon, the honest proof stack is: **MLSA University Community + named cities/venues + named tracks + organizing team + photos of prior MLSA/community workshops (captioned as those events, not as Abstracthon) + university partners if confirmed**. That is the Bolivia analog of AWS SCD stacking a first Student Community Day on Community Day history.
- What belongs in the deck (MLH + NamoID + Loopyah combined): name, dates, cities, format (vie virtual / sáb presencial / dom pitch), who organizes (MLSA University Community, named leads), which universities/venues are **confirmed**, track list mapped to sponsor industries, 3 tiers + custom, **what the sponsor gets to do**, and a report promise. Story of uniqueness = two-city Bolivia student builders + MLSA network + tracks in Finanzas/Educación/Comunicación/Ayuda tecnológica — not “we are passionate.”
- What community organizers often put that **does not convert**: mission/vision/values slides, “vibes,” undifferentiated “impacto social,” global program adjectives without local evidence, follower counts labeled as audience, logo walls of companies that were only *contacted*. MLH marketing buyers want photos + “how you are making a difference”; they still need attendee fit.
- Do **not** put a projected Abstracthon headcount in the deck unless the organizing team has a sourced number. MLH asks for projected attendance; Loopyah says if you cannot defend it, report current evidence and say you do not have a reliable prediction yet.

### Gaps
- No public, sourced Abstracthon 2026 registration or attendance figure was found. Do not invent one.
- No public MLSA Bolivia chapter attendance dashboard (workshops, members, universities) was found; MLSA CIT’s numbered workshops are an India example, not Bolivia proof.
- Microsoft’s 2024 “Updated Student Ambassadors Program” Tech Community article was blocked on fetch; milestone details (Alpha/Beta/Gold) come from search snippets only and should not be treated as verified from the live page.

## Which activations convert better than logo placement: branded challenge, jury, mentoring, API/workshop, booth, swag, hiring/collecting CVs, demo day presence?

### Takeaway
**Challenge ownership + workshop + on-floor mentoring + judging** is the conversion bundle. Logo, shoutout, and swag are table stakes. Resume books, booths, and demo-day/jury seats convert when they are tied to that bundle and to a post-event report (how many teams used the product, who won the track, opt-in CVs).

### Cited Findings
- MLH perks page, in this order of substance: **sponsored prize track/category** (“new people contributing in unique ways towards their product”); require a **representative who knows the challenge** so hackers can ask questions — “the best way to guarantee meaningful interactions”; **active mentoring** plus a sponsor Slack/Discord channel; **workshops** (schedule Friday/Saturday; Sunday workshops fail except pitching/submitting); **fun/games** as a non-technical alternative; **recruiting** via resume critiques, 1:1 recruiter fair, and **on-site interviews** in a private room if resumes were provided in advance. — [MLH: Potential Sponsor Perks](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/potential-sponsor-perks)
- ScarletHacks 2026 defines a **Branded Track** as the sponsor owning a problem space: “turns a portion of the event into your own **private innovation lab**.” Non-passive stack: (1) **45-minute launch workshop** to onboard the track; (2) **on-the-floor mentorship**; (3) **independent judging** owned by the sponsor. Other listed engagement: workshops, coffee chats, judging, opening/closing remarks, recap, on-site banners, swag. Tiers also sell **hacker resumes**, mentors, judges, custom award category. — [ScarletHacks 2026 packet](https://www.scarlethacks.com/SponsorshipPackage_ScarletHacks2026-4.pdf)
- Hack@Davidson explicitly prices the ladder: Bronze $1,000 = logo + shout-out + hacker profiles; Silver $3,000 adds booth, opt-in resume book, **workshop/tech talk**, mentors; Gold $5,000 adds keynote, **sponsored prize track around product/API**, judge nominations, T-shirt logo, **post-event report on attendance, projects, and use of your tools**. Headline: “More than a logo.” — [Hack@Davidson Sponsor](https://hackatdavidson.com/sponsor)
- HackRU: Bronze $1,500 is already “brand exposure **& API distribution**”; custom challenge + API is in general benefits; booths and recruiting materials appear at Silver; workshops at Gold; Title ($8,000) adds keynote, co-host, pre-event resume+GitHub, coffee chats, 1:1 talent. — [HackRU](https://sponsor.hackru.org/)
- Hack The Mountain 2026 (French-speaking student community, Canada) gates the high-value items: **own sponsored challenge (with prizes & jury)** and mentors appear at Terabyte $4,500; workshop 30 min at Megabyte $2,000; recruiting CVs / post-event email / interview rooms / on-site recruiters scale with tier. A-la-carte extras: social takeover $300, pre-hackathon workshop $300. — [Hack The Mountain 2026 packet](https://hackthemountain.ca/SponsorPackage_HackTheMountain_EN_2026.pdf)
- Hack Western a-la-carte prices (13th edition, CAD): coffee chats $1,500; booth $2,500; **workshop $3,000**; onsite interviews $1,500; individual social post $500. Workshops and interviews are priced **above** a social post by 3–6×. Custom API/prize, resume filter, final judge, and tailored stats sit in higher tiers. — [Hack Western packet](https://www.hackwestern.com/shared/sponsors/sponsors.pdf)
- Hacktech 2026 (Caltech, tentative): Bronze $1K / Silver $2K / Gold $5K / Platinum $10K. Recruiting column: distribute materials, receive resumes, send recruiters, **onsite interviews**. Additional: booth, exclusive Caltech-student hour, **company theme / prize / workshop**, send judges, keynote. “ALL DETAILS ARE NEGOTIABLE.” Judge copy: judges “interview hackers, demo their creations, and learn more about their design process… a great way to **identify and recruit** the best talent.” — [Hacktech 2026 tentative packet](https://hack.caltech.edu/2026/hacktech_2026_sponsor_tentative.pdf)
- Berlin 2026 commercial packet names three stacked tiers: Supporting = logo + social + community presence + **Demo Day presence**; Challenge = own branded challenge from a real business problem, briefing, team working two days on the task, **jury seat at Demo Day**, recap and follow-up access; Naming = naming rights, keynote, exclusive talent access. — [Hackation Berlin 2026 infopaket](https://hackation.de/downloads/hackathon-berlin-2026/berlin-sponsoring-infopaket-en.pdf)
- The App Blueprint Mobile AI Hackathon prospectus lists activations in this order: banner/expo, **sponsored workshop**, **category prize**, **mentor access**, talent pipeline (opt-in resumes), campaign reach. Run-your-own-prize and main-prize judge seat are Platinum-only. — [App Blueprint sponsor deck](https://theappblueprint.io/hackathon-sponsor-deck.pdf)
- AngelHack: “Having a branded banner at your table is the **bare minimum**.” Swag creates familiarity (stickers especially). The converting move is a **challenge** aligned to a business pain, with API integration **required to win the prize**; technical mentors, docs, Slack, optional pre-hack webinar. “Some partners have said they got more in one hackathon weekend than in months of expensive R&D.” Afterward: thank-you, imagery, survey, metrics vs the sponsor’s KPIs. — [AngelHack](https://angelhack.com/blog/be-an-all-star-sponsor-at-your-next-hackathon/)
- NamoID: “The perks sponsors value most are often the ones that **cost you almost nothing**: a prize track, a workshop slot, a featured API.” “The sponsor challenge is the **highest-leverage item**.” Offer it in every tier above Community. Deliver a results summary within a week: attendance, projects on their product, photos, social reach, 1–2 standout teams. — [NamoID](https://namoid.in/blog/how-to-get-sponsors-for-your-hackathon)
- Gupta: Track/Challenge tier = named track, dedicated prize, judge on that panel. Infrastructure/in-kind = logo + welcome-kit mention (weaker). Close-out within a week: how many teams used the API, who won the track, recap link. — [Gupta](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- AWS SCD Bolivia 2026: Silver USD 300 is essentially **logo + stage mention** (the non-converting floor). Gold USD 500 adds booth + jobs + report. Platinum/Host add **workshop, branded challenge, opt-in CVs**. In-kind (food, venue, swag, credits, production) is credited at market price toward a tier. — [AWS SCD Bolivia](https://bolivia.studentcommunity.day/en/sponsor-deck)
- HackAdton Puebla 2026 (inter-university marketing hack, Mexico) treats **jury, talks, patrocinio, mentoría** as **parallel conversion paths**, not one logo package — brands can enter as judges or mentors without buying a title. Calendar: online kickoff/briefing, later in-person pitch. — [HackAdton](https://hackadton.com.mx/)
- Northland CSR piece: sending **volunteer mentors** is framed as the highest-emotion employee-engagement activation; prizes/tools are the product-adoption path; logo-only is the financial-sponsorship minimum. — [Northland CSR](https://northlandhackathon.com/corporate-social-responsibility-tech-education)

| Activation | Why sponsors pay up for it | Typical tier |
|---|---|---|
| Branded challenge / prize track / API | Hours of product use; prototypes; case-study content | Mid–top; sometimes in all paid tiers (HackRU, NamoID advice) |
| Launch workshop / API workshop | Captive teaching slot; adoption on-ramp | Mid–top; MLH: Fri/Sat only |

### Inferences
- For Abstracthon’s weekend shape: put sponsor **workshops on Friday virtual and Saturday**; Sunday is pitch/jury (MLH: Sunday workshops fail). Demo-day presence on Sunday is valuable **if** the sponsor already mentored/judged their track.
- Abstracthon’s four tracks are a native **challenge-ownership** offer: a bank does not need “logo on the website”; it needs **Finanzas** or a named sub-challenge, a Saturday mentor, and a Sunday jury seat. Educación maps to CSR + edtech; Comunicación to media/telco; Ayuda tecnológica to platform/API vendors who want cross-track usage.
- Swag is not worthless, but packets never make it the reason to buy. Selling “your logo on the shirt” as the headline is the community-organizer failure mode.
- Collecting CVs only converts if **opt-in** is explicit (Hack@Davidson, AWS SCD Bolivia, App Blueprint). Pitch “libro de CVs de quienes acepten,” not a dump of the full registration list.
- What to put on the **tier slide**: do-column (challenge, workshop, mentor, judge, CVs, booth, report) above see-column (logo size, posts, T-shirt). MLH: lowest tier still includes **general + publicity + recruitment**, not logo-only.

### Gaps
- No A/B or sponsor-survey study was found that measures conversion (renewal, hires, API DAU) of challenge vs booth vs swag at student hackathons. Ranking above is inferred from **price and copy in 2024–2026 packets**, plus MLH/AngelHack qualitative guidance.
- Hacktech PDF fetch timed out; details above rely on search extraction of the same URL.

## How should a first or early-edition hackathon talk about reach without inflating numbers?

### Takeaway
Separate **confirmed operations**, **interest**, **channel followings**, and **attendance** into labeled, dated lines. Never add them. If there is no history, say so, show the evidence you do have, and sell a **bounded activation with a report**, not a guaranteed crowd.

### Cited Findings
- Loopyah (2026) is the most operational source on this exact problem:
  - Do not merge tickets, interest-list, and followers into “our audience.” **Report each channel separately**; state that overlap is unknown and figures **must not be added**.
  - Paid/registered admissions ≠ unique people ≠ completed attendance. Followers are an account audience, possibly off-market.
  - Date every figure. An undated number is hard to assess.
  - Forecasts: you may show **capacity** and **planning cases**, but do not assign fake probabilities. “We don’t have a reliable attendance prediction yet. We’ll send a dated update on [date]” is an acceptable sponsor-facing sentence.
  - If the sponsor demands a guaranteed minimum, understand the remedy or **decline and sell a smaller activity**.
  - US FTC small-business advertising guidance is cited as an editorial check: would a reasonable reader take away a **stronger claim than the evidence supports**? A slide titled “Our attendees” over follower counts misleads. A logo wall of companies only contacted implies confirmed backing.
  - Partner channels: “one inclusion in the August newsletter” ≠ “access to all members.” A speaker’s following is not yours. A venue booking is not permission to email its customers.
  - Before tickets exist, **do not present an interest list as a substitute for purchases**. You can describe programme, research, and confirmed promo, and/or postpone the large ask.
  — [Loopyah](https://loopyah.com/blog/selling/first-event-sponsorship)
- NamoID: expected attendance is “the number sponsors buy,” but first edition should be **realistic**, backed by college brand and faculty. After the event, the report (not the forecast) unlocks year two. — [NamoID](https://namoid.in/blog/how-to-get-sponsors-for-your-hackathon)
- Unstop: a 5,000-registration hackathon that produces three hires is not more successful, for hiring ROI, than a 500-registration event that produces three hires. Participation **rate** (registered who actually complete) and submission quality beat raw registrations. — [Unstop hiring](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-vs-internship-hiring); [Unstop metrics](https://unstop.com/blog/hackathon-success-metrics)
- AWS SCD Bolivia shows the honest first-edition pattern: label the new event as first, publish **prior related-community** numbers with notes (“2025 registrations are approximate. 2026 figures are final.”), and sell **universities + city + report**, not a blended “reach” mega-number. — [AWS SCD Bolivia](https://bolivia.studentcommunity.day/en/sponsor-deck)
- Gupta: put **event name, exact date, and expected headcount up front** because “vendors triage by scale before anything else” — this **pressures** organizers to invent a number. Counterweight with Loopyah: if you state a headcount, it must be a labeled projection with method, not a promise. — [Gupta](https://guptadeepak.com/hackathon-sponsorship-guide/)
- MLH still wants projected attendance on the prospectus. Treat that as “state a projection **and its basis**,” not “invent a round number for the slide.” — [MLH Prospectus](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/getting-sponsorship/sponsorship-prospectus)
- Hack Western’s mature packet reports **320 total participants** and **82 projects** from the *previous* edition, plus demographic breakdowns — i.e. **last year’s actuals**, not this year’s hope. — [Hack Western](https://www.hackwestern.com/shared/sponsors/sponsors.pdf)

**Metrics and proof points that work at small / first-edition / few-hundred / 2–3 cities (use only if true and dated):**

- Named cities and venues (confirmed vs under discussion).
- Named universities or student groups **that have agreed to promote or host**.
- Organizing-team names, roles, and prior events they actually ran (with those events’ real counts, labeled).
- MLSA / university-community affiliation, plus any **count of ambassadors or prior workshops** that can be sourced.
- Track list and which sponsor can own which track.
- Format hours (virtual Friday vs in-person Saturday vs Sunday pitch) — this is operational reach (attention windows), not headcount.
- Photos and short clips from prior community events, **captioned with event name and year**.
- If registration is open: **current registrations as of DATE**, not a year-end guess.
- If not open: say so.
- Promise a post-event pack: actual attendance by city, projects submitted, teams that used sponsor X, opt-in CV count, photos, social posts **with impressions if the platform provides them** (do not add across networks).
- Other confirmed sponsors (MLH: “who else is sponsoring” is a top concern).

**Phrases that inflate and should not appear:**
- Adding Instagram + LinkedIn + Discord + “community” into one “alcance.”
- “Cientos / miles de desarrolladores” with no source.
- “El hackathon más grande de Bolivia” with no comparison data.
- Using another city’s AWS/GDG/WTM attendance as if it were Abstracthon’s.
- Showing Cochabamba as a public city if it is only internal.

### Inferences
- Abstracthon should talk about reach as **two confirmed in-person nodes (Santa Cruz, Oruro) + a Friday virtual on-ramp**, an MLSA university network, and four industry tracks — then stop. If Cochabamba is not public, do not put it on the sponsor slide.
- Price the first edition like AWS SCD Bolivia’s bounded packages (hundreds of USD, activation-based) rather than Hack Western’s CAD $6k–$15k, unless a sponsor is buying a national API program.
- Year-one commercial deal: **discount or smaller package in exchange for a testimonial + metrics the sponsor will let you show next year** (NamoID). That is how small events become able to show numbers.

- No standard audited attendance methodology used by LATAM student hackathons was found (Loopyah cites ABC business-event standards as a *principle*, not as something these events use).
- Social-impression benchmarks for Bolivian student-tech events were not found in public 2022–2026 sources.

## Deck highlights vs community filler (synthesis for the report-writer)

**Put in the sponsor-call deck (grounded in MLH prospectus + 2026 packets + Loopyah):**
1. Event name, dates, cities, format (vie virtual / sáb IRL / dom pitch).
2. Who organizes (MLSA University Community; named team).
3. Unique bridge: two-city student builders; four industry tracks; university-community access that a conference booth or a Facebook post does not give.
4. Buyer jobs as columns, not slogans: hiring, brand with developers, product/API feedback, RSE-educación, student pipeline, **challenge ownership**, workshops.
5. Three tiers that escalate **doing** (workshop → challenge → jury → CVs → report), not logo size.
6. Proof that is real: photos of prior MLSA/community events (labeled), universities/venues confirmed, other sponsors if any.
7. A report promise (attendance by city, projects, tool usage, opt-in contacts) with a deadline (AWS Bolivia uses 15 days; NamoID uses one week).

**Leave out or demote (does not convert):**
- Mission / visión / valores as opening slides.
- “Energía,” “vibes,” “el más vibrante.”
- Inflated or blended reach.
- Logo-only as the hero benefit.
- Invented audience size.
- Captioning other events as Abstracthon.
- Cochabamba if it is not public.
- CSR language with no student-pipeline or employee-mentor mechanism.

**Do not invent Abstracthon N.** If a projection is required because MLH-style prospectuses include one, it must come from the organizing team with a method and a date — it is not in these notes.
